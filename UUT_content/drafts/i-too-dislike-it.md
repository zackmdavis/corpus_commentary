> I too, dislike it: there are things that are important beyond all this fiddle.  
> Reading it, however, with a perfect contempt for it, one discovers that there is in it after all,  
> a place for the genuine.  
> Hands that can grasp, eyes that can dilate, hair that can rise  
> if it must, these things are important not because a  
>  
> high-sounding interpretation can be put upon them but because  
> they are useful; when they become so derivative as to become  
>  unintelligible, the same thing may be said for all of us—that we do not admire what we cannot understand.  
>
> —["Poetry"](https://poets.org/poem/poetry) by Marianne Moore
