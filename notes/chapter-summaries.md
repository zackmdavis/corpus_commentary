## Ch. 4 — Where to Draw the Boundaries: The Philosophy of Categorization (`study/04-categorization.html`; draft 2, 2026-10-17, revised after Fable review; ~10,400 words by crude count)

**Thesis as written.** Davis's categorization program defends one distinction, stated more precisely over 2018–2025 (subspaces → mutual information → squared error → causal graphs): values may choose which features a category must help predict (the "first kind of value-dependence," term borrowed from Davis's draft); given that choice, the distribution of things *constrains* the boundary (not "fixes": Kleinberg 2002; gjm's ties). The distinction is correct and exposes a gap in Alexander 2014: his §IV conclusion needs an unargued premise about what kind of category *woman* is (closer to a border than to *fish*). Alexander's Dec 2019 note and Yudkowsky's Sept 2020 clarification (as Davis quotes it) concede the narrow point. The stronger claims are located by text and date: the 2019 rule ("Appeals to consequences are invalid"; "not allowed to care"), the 2020 intuition (memoir: squiggly boundaries' only realistic use is fraud), the 2021 classification sentence and title, and polemics (2018 "universal socially-mandatory lies"; 2021 Great Teacher; 2022 "absurd lies"). **The 2021 capstone essay itself already holds the norm of accounting** ("deception can be rational"; "notice that what you're doing is deceiving people"; meat substitutes "maybe that's the right thing to do"); there is no peak-then-retreat arc. Assessment of the two claims: (i) *departure as deception* holds where a departure is concealed and listeners decode the old convention (the 2018 sticker case), not for announced departures or pronouns (Davis's own 2022 point); (ii) *the rule against appeals to consequences* is only as sharp as the two-kinds distinction (no rigorous reduction of relevance). **Thesis 2 is argued here:** the rule is defensible as a rule-consequentialist norm (Hooker), and Davis's coordination argument ("Speaking Truth to Power": only x = ∞ and x = 0 are Schelling points) has that structure; but the slope runs along map accuracy, a disclosed departure keeps both facts on the map, so the argument supports an exceptionless rule against *concealment* (also easier to enforce than a rule about motives), i.e. the norm of accounting. "Self-Identity Is a Schelling Point" shows a changed convention can be stable. On *woman*: the two real categories are developmental sex and social gender (an interactive kind built on perceived sex, which is an estimate of developmental sex); by the framework's own lights the dispute is first-kind value-dependence (legitimate), a metalinguistic negotiation (Plunkett & Sundell): favorable to the framework, unfavorable to the polemic. Davis's best reply (social gender's base property is sex: Gresham, Ásta's "base property" slot) rests on an empirical claim about what speakers use the word for; cata's point (appearance-based classification of dolphins vs. pronouns). ## Ch. 5 — Algorithms of Deception: Honesty Without Intent, and the Attribution Problem (`study/05-deception.html`; draft 2, 2026-10-17, revised after review (requested Fable; ran on Opus); ~7,350 words by crude count, ~3% over ceiling)

**Thesis as written.** Davis's functional account of deception (communication optimized/selected to worsen a receiver's predictions, intent irrelevant: "Algorithms of Deception!" 2019; "Algorithmic Intent" 2020) is *defensible*, picks out a real class the intentional concept misses, and has close counterparts in the signaling literature. It is **receiver-relative**: deception is relative to a reference listener who decodes signals at their conventional meaning, which "Maybe Lying Can't Exist?!" (2020) grounds in Skyrms–Barrett (propositional content fixed in the common-interest subgame; deception parasitic). Davis adopts the Skyrmsian framework (via Krstić's survey; sender benefit as systematicity), declines Fallis & Lewis's bullet on counteradapted mimicry, bites Martínez's bullet ("Comment on 'Deception as Cooperation'," 2021: "maps optimized to reflect… and maps optimized to control"), and drops sender benefit for "deception," keeping it for "fraud." Krstić 2023 reached a similar view (not "independent evidence," just not idiosyncratic); Krstić's function is selected-effects (Garson), Davis mixes selection (MLCE) and disposition (AI's robustness to perturbation); the difference matters for attribution. **Strongest objection, partly Davis's own:** intent bears on epistemic repair (AI: "the real map for ourselves, and a fake map for our victims"; ABF: conscious deception "puts up much stronger resistance"; Wei Dai); the account absorbs it as a mechanism difference but not its ABF claim that the difference is "uninteresting." Applications: "Heads I Win" (disclosure turns distortion into missing information; cata; footnote symmetry test; whether Davis's advocacy discloses its filter → Ch. 7); "Firming Up" (Alexander concedes not-lying ≠ honesty but his objection to the training-cost claim stands; Davis's "strategic political concession" self-described as a small instance of the distortion, mitigated by disclosure); "Stupidity and Dishonesty" (Davis concedes richer models; omitted-variable problem; definitions don't license "not for accuracy" → "against accuracy"); "Communication Requires…" (Ninety-Three; Lopez; post already cites Lachmann et al.); "Speaking Truth to Power" (Kaas deflected, Vaniver unanswered, "rigged" to Wei Dai, Davis's self-set test of counter-narrative examples; structure survives as a rule against concealment, Ch. 4 §8); "Bounded Distrust"; "Social Punishment" (unconsidered option: disclosed non-publication, losing information without bias → Ch. 6). §4 Williams: Davis's criterion is broadened Sincerity; trust is the receiver's model of the filter; the open question is whether trust should outrun evidence (Sabien, Ch. 6).
