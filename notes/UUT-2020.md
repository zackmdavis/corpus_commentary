### Book Review: Charles Murray's *Human Diversity: The Biology of Gender, Race, and Class*
- Path: `UUT_content/2020/book-review-human-diversity.md` · 2020-04-28 · commentary (Charles Murray, race, sex differences, intelligence, stigma, …) · 11017 w · http://unremediatedgender.space/2020/Apr/book-review-human-diversity/
- Summary: Part 1 (the review he'd "prefer to write"): Cohen's d; aggregation of small effects (96% face-sex accuracy); sex differences in personality/interests (larger in richer countries; William Buckner's WEIRD-sample criticism noted); Johnson & Bouchard (g masks dimensions; matches his own 2019 post); ancestry clustering (Li et al. 2008, K-populations) as construct "doing _cognitive work_"; standing variation, introgression; ACE twin models; small shared-environment effects; heritability caveats (heads, sun/shade plants, red-hair caste); Plomin vs. Turkheimer on polygenic scores; Appendix 2 presents Blanchard–Bailey–Lawrence–Littman as fact — "basically _correct_" but should have mentioned Serano or Veale. Part 2 ("irrelevant"): no "Actual Social Science" because beliefs about groups face incentives to deceive and self-deceive; Murray's "no monsters in the closet" positioning is ineffective ("theistic evolution"); "Nothing we learn will threaten human equality properly understood" is suspicious in form. Blank slate as Schelling point against oppression — explains "dread doors": undermining it could slide to worse equilibria; Galileo/Darwin analogy (authorities were right to fear). Discusses *The Bell Curve*; black–white IQ gap d≈1; personal: 85% Ashkenazi (23andMe), WISC-III 131 at age 10; 800m 2:08.3 in high school. Standard caveats on between-group causation; ways environmental hypotheses are testable (item characteristic curves, SES control cuts gap by a third, skin-color vs. ancestry studies) — explicitly *not* a lit review ("That's someone else's blog"). His reason: "I want _a world that's not lying to me_"; structural oppression and group differences can coexist (strength d≈2.6 and patriarchy). Lavender-humans thought experiment. Pinker's principle (quoted by Murray) that "individuals should not be judged or constrained by the average properties of their group" isn't literally coherent epistemics (Bayes; Emacs shirt vs. sex; Nelson et al. 1990 height judgments); renormalized egalitarianism = seek finer-grained information. Finer-grained stereotype of himself ("IQ 130, mid-to-low Conscientiousness and Agreeableness, high Openness, left-libertarian American Jewish atheist autogynephilic male computer programmers"). Bites bullet: "intellectual ability _is_ a component of human worth." Defends embryo selection; Dawkins episode. Xenosystems "five stages of HBD"; "stuck halfway between Stage 4 and 5"; resolves to reorganize "at the other Schelling point of _just tell the goddamned truth_."
- Themes: charles-murray, human-biodiversity, race-and-iq, sex-differences, heritability, blank-slate, schelling-points, statistical-discrimination, egalitarianism-renormalized, eugenics/embryo-selection, social-science-incentives, biography
- Xrefs: LW linkpost 2020-04-28 ("A Book Review", self-mocking intro). UUT drafts `the-causality-of-ethnic-differences-in-covid-19-outcomes-is-an-open-empirical-question.md`, `imperfect-trait-measurements-regress-to-the-mean.md`. For critique phase: claims about race/IQ require outside literature (incl. critics: Turkheimer, Nisbett, Flynn, Kaplan; and the hereditarian side); Davis explicitly disclaims expertise on causality of group differences.
- Quotes:
> The construct is doing _cognitive work_, concisely summarizing statistical regularities in the dataset (which is _too large_ for humans to hold in their heads all at once): a map that reflects a territory.
> Murray presents the Blanchard–Bailey–Lawrence–Littman view as fact, which I think is basically _correct_, but a more comprehensive treatment (which I concede may be too much too hope for from a mere Appendix) would have at least _mentioned_ alternative views
> We do not have a discipline of Actual Social Science. Possibly because we're not smart enough to do it, but perhaps more so because we're not smart enough to _want_ to do it.
> I think this strategy is sympathetic but [ultimately ineffective](http://zackmdavis.net/blog/2016/08/ineffective-deconversion-pitch/). Murray is trying to have it both ways: challenging the orthodoxy, while denying the possibility of any [unfortunate implications](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/UnfortunateImplications) of the orthodoxy being false.
> And that's where the blank slate doctrine absolutely _shines_—it's the [Schelling point](/2019/Oct/self-identity-is-a-schelling-point/#schelling-point) for preventing group conflicts!
> In analogy, Galileo and Darwin weren't _trying_ to undermine Christianity—they had much more interesting things to think about—but religious authorities were _right_ to fear heliocentrism and evolution: if the prevailing coordination equilibrium depends on lies, then telling the truth _is_ a threat and it _is_ disloyal.
> In mentioning these arguments in passing, I'm _not_ trying to provide a comprehensive lit review on the causality of group IQ differences.
> I don't want to be complicit with hatred or oppression. I want to stay loyal to the underlying egalitarian–individualist axiology that makes the blank slate doctrine _sound like a good idea_. But I also want to understand reality, to make sense of things. I want _a world that's not lying to me_.
> But can I take this _literally_ as the _exact_ statement of a moral principle? _Technically?_—no! That's actually not how epistemology works!
> I used to be a naïve egalitarian. I was very passionate about it. I was eighteen years old.
> If our means of measuring individuals aren't reliable or cheap enough, such that we still end up using prior information from immutable demographic categories, that's a problem of grave moral seriousness—but in light of the [_mathematical laws_](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eY45uCCX7DdwJ4Jha/no-one-can-exempt-you-from-rationality-s-laws) governing reasoning under uncertainty, it's a problem that realistically needs to be solved with _better tests_ and _better signals_, not by _pretending not to have a prior_.
> But if someone says, "Zack M. Davis? Oh, he's one of those IQ 130, [mid-to-low Conscientiousness and Agreeableness, high Openness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_personality_traits), left-libertarian American Jewish atheist autogynephilic male computer programmers; you know what _they're_ like," my response is to nod and say, "Yeah, pretty much."
> _Yes_, intellectual ability _is_ a component of human worth! Maybe that's putting it baldly, but I think the _alternative_ is obviously senseless.
> The idea that people are unequal in ways that matter is [legitimately too horrifying to contemplate](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/faHbrHuPziFH7Ef7p/why-are-individual-iq-differences-ok), so liberals [deny the inequality](/2017/Dec/theres-a-land-that-i-see-or-the-spirit-of-intervention/), and conservatives deny [that it matters](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/NG4XQEL5PTyguDMff/but-it-doesn-t-matter).
> So it is with respect for the good works enabled by the anti-oppression Schelling point belief, that I set my sights on reorganizing at the [other Schelling point of _just tell the goddamned truth_](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/tCwresAuSvk867rzH/speaking-truth-to-power-is-a-schelling-point)—not in spite of the consequences, but because of the consequences of what good people can do when we're fully informed.
