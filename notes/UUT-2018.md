### Don't Negotiate With Terrorist Memeplexes
- Path: `UUT_content/2018/dont-negotiate-with-terrorist-memeplexes.md` · 2018-01-23 · commentary (discourse, game theory) · 1969 w · http://unremediatedgender.space/2018/Jan/dont-negotiate-with-terrorist-memeplexes/
- Summary: Epigraph Kipling's "Dane-Geld." Memetics: ideas that threaten consequences for disbelief spread better. Example meme: "You are a member of marginalized identity group Y; anyone who notices facts that could be construed to call this narrative into question is thereby hurting you" — friends then pretend not to notice, like paying ransom. Autobiography: "the beautiful feeling at the center of my life" — knows he's male but doesn't want to identify with it; brain doesn't distinguish "people I want to be _with_ and people I want to be _like_"; long self-identified with the word "autogynephilia" while assuming the theory false; Portland 2016 reading (Kay Brown, Lawrence, *Nevada*) convinced him he has "the same underlying psychological condition that leads people to transition" and his story "may even be _typical_." Should be in sex-ed books at 15. The "moment of liberating clarity": an IM argument with a cis male acquaintance who said to wait 25–30 years, that autogynephilia isn't real, "you were a girl and misdiagnosed it" — "It _felt like_ I was talking to an AI designed to maximize the number of trans people." The "Orwellian horror": doubting his social circle would back him in public. Supports trans people's access to hormones/surgery and pronouns, but "_my life_ is not hate speech." Policy: will correct factual errors; on offense, "I just really, fundamentally _do not care_ anymore."
- Themes: memetics, emotional-blackmail, offense-vs-truth, autogynephilia (self-description), rationalist-community, policy-on-offense, pretend-not-to-notice
- Xrefs: AAL 2016 "Late-Onset" (same phrase); UUT 2023 "A Hill of Validity…" (expanded narrative).
- Quotes:
> A human who has accepted—who has been _taken hostage by_—this idea, will feel genuine pain and distress whenever anyone notices facts that could be construed to call the narrative into question. And so the human's friends, who love and care about them, will dutifully make sure to _pretend not to notice_ any inconvenient facts, and socially punish anyone who doesn't _pretend not to notice_, in order to avoid hurting their friend.
> So, there's a thing about me, possibly even _the_ thing about me, where there is this beautiful feeling at the center of my life that has shaped me more than almost anything else, where obviously I know that I am in fact male, but I don't want to _identify_ with that fact; I [want to believe](/2017/Dec/theres-a-land-that-i-see-or-the-spirit-of-intervention/) that I could be female and still be the same person in all the ways that matter, and this sentiment feels tied to my sexuality, as if my brain just doesn't draw that much of a distinction between people I want to be _with_ and people I want to be _like_.
> This is _really important information_! This is _not_ the sort of thing someone should have to piece together themselves at age 28!
> He said, Yes, you were a girl and misdiagnosed it; that's the simplest explanation of the facts.
> It _felt like_ I was talking to an AI designed to maximize the number of trans people.
> Obviously I'm totally in favor of trans _people_ having access to the hormones and surgeries that they want, and having their preferred pronouns respected. That's just individual freedom and basic politeness.
> But _my life_ is not hate speech.
> I don't care. I just really, fundamentally _do not care_ anymore.

### Blegg Mode
- Path: `UUT_content/2018/blegg-mode.md` · 2018-02-01 · commentary (categorization, deniably allegorical, epistemology) · 790 w · http://unremediatedgender.space/2018/Feb/blegg-mode/
- Summary: Fanfiction of Yudkowsky's blegg/rube parable ("A Human's Guide to Words"): hard, blue-furred, egg-ish objects turn out to be sanded, fur-covered rubes; narrator invokes SSC's "categories were made for man" to call them "adapted bleggs"; 90% contain palladium (like 98% of rubes, 2% of bleggs). Narrator nonetheless mentally treats them as a third category. Conjecture: categories are worth reifying when cheap-to-detect features correlate with expensive, decision-relevant features (2×2 diagram, viewed: easily-observed feature × decision-relevant hidden feature; "adapted rube-to-bleggs" in the yes/yes cell = "worth a new category"). Purple bleggs (observable, not decision-relevant) and palladium bleggs (decision-relevant, not observable) don't warrant categories.
- Themes: categorization, blegg-rube, deniable-allegory (trans women), cheap-features-predict-hidden-features, scott-alexander
- Xrefs: LW linkpost 2019-03-11 (`LW_content/2019/blegg-mode.md`) — check LW comments; Yudkowsky "Disguised Queries"; AAL 2019 "Where to Draw the Boundaries?".
- Quotes:
> "Hold on," you say, "I'm not sure we should be disrespecting that effort by calling them _rubes_. [The categories were made for man, not man for the categories](http://web.archive.org/web/20200610230130/https://slatestarcodex.com/2014/11/21/the-categories-were-made-for-man-not-man-for-the-categories/): there's no rule of sorting saying that we should call them rubes, and there are plenty of rules of human decency saying that we should call them bleggs.
> After some thought, you conjecture that it probably has something to do with having cheap-to-detect features that correlate with more-expensive-to-detect features that are decision-relevant with respect to the agent's goals—
> In contrast, adapted bleggs are _both_ easily identifiable _and_ the difference matters to your decisionmaking: a distinction that makes a difference, something your brain wants to have an efficient representation so that you can attend to it.

### The Categories Were Made for Man to Make Predictions
- Path: `UUT_content/2018/the-categories-were-made-for-man-to-make-predictions.md` · 2018-02-23 · commentary (categorization, Scott Alexander, sex differences, two-type taxonomy, whale metaphors) · 6525 w · http://unremediatedgender.space/2018/Feb/the-categories-were-made-for-man-to-make-predictions/
- Summary: Reply to Scott Alexander's "The Categories Were Made for Man, Not Man for the Categories" (SSC 2014). Agrees definitions are evaluated by usefulness and no categorization is uniquely "true," but "that doesn't release us from our sacred duty to describe what's actually true"; categories do cognitive work via cluster structure in configuration space (whales as mammals vs. *dagim* in different subspaces; Lincoln's dog-tail riddle). Alexander's trans argument (accept "an unexpected man or two" to save a life; hair-dryer OCD analogy) is a motte; disanalogy: redefining "woman" affects everyone who uses top-20 nouns (aggregated "dust specks"). A non-question-begging pro-trans argument appeals to passing/System-1 perception — works "_because_ and _to the extent that_ transitioning actually works." Two-type taxonomy: androphilic early-onset (likely brain-intersex; fraternal birth order) vs. majority non-androphilic late-onset (not intersex or mildly); allows readers to accept two types without AGP causation. Clusters figure (viewed): purple trans-women cluster matches blue (male) on x₁,x₂ and pink (female) on x₃; boundary choice depends on which variables you care to predict. SSC 2018 survey: 13.4% of women respondents trans if trans women are women. Quotes two female friends on women-only events and restrooms as safe havens; complaints don't dictate policy but are real stakes. As transhumanist/individualist: right to transition and right to freedom of association (sex-segregated spaces). APA pronoun stickers: "If you need a sticker to get people to gender you correctly, _your transition has failed_"; "All you can do is incentivize them to lie." Emperor Norton parable: playing along with a friend's delusion isn't kindness; recounts his own 2017 delusions (Gender Czar, transgender mafia, acausal economy). Ends with the advisor admitting "You're not actually Emperor" and helping plan.
- Themes: categorization, scott-alexander, cluster-structure-of-thingspace, two-type-taxonomy, sex-segregated-spaces, pronouns, emperor-norton, transhumanism, madness (2017 delusions), gerrymandering
- Xrefs: Ozy's reply "Man Should Allocate Some More Categories" (2018-06-18, thingofthings) — reply drafted ~4800 w (see Metablogging; draft `UUT_content/drafts/i-mean-yes-i-agree-that-man-should-allocate-some-more-categories-but.md`). `UUT_content/pages/ancillary/categories-scatterplot-source.md`. SSC original to fetch. Later: AAL 2019 "Where to Draw the Boundaries?"; UUT 2023 memoir.
- Quotes:
> All of this is entirely correct—and thus, an excellent [motte](http://web.archive.org/web/20200529221511/https://slatestarcodex.com/2014/11/03/all-in-all-another-brick-in-the-motte/) for the less honest sort of _Slate Star Codex_ reader to appeal to when they want to obfuscate and disrupt discussions about empirical reality by insisting on gerrymandered redefinitions of everyday concepts.
> But this is just giving up _way_ too easily. The map is not the territory, and many very different kinds of maps can correspond to the territory in different ways—we have geographical maps, political maps, road maps, globes, _&c._—but that doesn't mean _no map is in error_.
> Rationalists can't insist on using the one true categorization system, because it turns out that—in all philosophical strictness—no such thing exists. But that doesn't release us from our sacred duty to describe what's actually true.
> In terms of the Lincoln riddle: you _can_ call a tail a leg, but you can't stop people from _noticing_ that out of a dog's five legs, one of them is different from the others.
> That there exists an element of subjectivity in what you choose to pay attention to, doesn't negate the fact that there _is_ a structured empirical reality to be described—and not all descriptions of it are equally compact.
> The words _man_ and _woman_ [are top-20 nouns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_common_words_in_English#Nouns)! People need those nouns to describe their experiences!
> Well, no. But the point is that this is an _empirical_ argument for why successfully socially-transitioned trans people fit into _existing_ concepts of gender, _not_ a redefinition of top-20 nouns by fiat in order to avoid hurting someone's feelings. It works _because_ and _to the extent that_ transitioning actually works.
> To avoid the main ideas of this post getting mired in _unnecessary_ controversy, I'd like to emphasize that it's possible to reject the hypothesis that autogynephilia is the _cause_ of the second type, while [still agreeing that](https://thingofthings.wordpress.com/2017/04/18/against-blanchardianism/) there observationally seem to be _at least_ two types of trans women

> All you can do is incentivize them to lie.
> I don't _want_ to be "anti-trans." I can easily imagine _myself_ transitioning (I've [already experimented](/tag/hrt-diary/) with the relevant drugs), in a nearby possible past in which my analogue was braver and read different books in a different order, or a nearby possible future in which the technology gets better.
> This is not rationality. This isn't even kindness. We're _smarter_ than this.

### Reply to *The Unit of Caring* on Adult Human Females
- Path: `UUT_content/2018/reply-to-the-unit-of-caring-on-adult-human-females.md` · 2018-04-19 · commentary (categorization, Kelsey Piper, terminology, transhumanism) · 3758 w · http://unremediatedgender.space/2018/Apr/reply-to-the-unit-of-caring-on-adult-human-females/
- Summary: Epigraph Alicorn: "Thou shalt not strike terms from others' expressive vocabulary without suitable replacement." Reply to *The Unit of Caring* (Kelsey Piper's Tumblr; tagged "Kelsey Piper") asking for any justification of "adult human female." Good property: non-circularity (vs. "zorplebobben are people who identify as zorplebobben"). Trans-inclusive sense as a principled broadening ("adult human females, and also adult human males who have undergone interventions to resemble adult human females sufficiently closely"). Rebuts three objections: (1) categories are social decisions — not all equally useful (yellow-things biology); (2) future morphological freedom — irrelevant to the present; technology is specific, hard-won (cites Yudkowsky "Changing Emotions"); (3) cell-for-cell identical woman — Sorites; conditional answer: if psych sex differences exist and most trans women aren't female-minded, perfect bodies still come with male-distribution psychologies; "Honestly, I lean towards _Yes_, but I can at least _see the argument_." Fictional Alice/Bob dialogues (track meet; meetup with "literally all of us ... trans"; Dhejne et al. 2011 Swedish violent-crime hazard ratios: vs. cis women 18.1, vs. cis men 0.8; death-redefinition dialogue). Zinnia Jones's "female penises" quote. "assigned sex at birth (as if the doctors made a _mistake_!)."
- Themes: definitions-and-categories, non-circularity, kelsey-piper, alicorns-maxim, morphological-freedom, sorites, dhejne-2011, sex-differences, transhumanism
- Xrefs: External: theunitofcaring.tumblr.com post (fetch if possible); Dhejne et al. 2011 PLoS ONE (check the claimed table figures in research phase). UUT 2025 "Interview with Kelsey Piper".
- Quotes:
> I would say that a notable good property of the "adult human female" definition is _non-circularity_: we can articulate membership tests that do a pretty good job of narrowing down which entities _do_ and _do not_ belong to the category we're trying to talk about, _without_ appealing to the category itself.
> If someone told you that zorplebobben are people who identify as zorplebobben, you would probably have questions about what that means!
> Thus we can legitimately end up with a _non_-circular trans-inclusive sense of the word _women_: "adult human females, and also adult human males who have undergone interventions to resemble adult human females sufficiently closely so that they can be taken as such socially."
> there's something _profoundly frustrating_ about [Blue Tribe](http://web.archive.org/web/20200623015648/https://slatestarcodex.com/2014/09/30/i-can-tolerate-anything-except-the-outgroup/) culture's axiomatic insistence that certain inferences _must not_ be made, that certain conceptual distinctions must not be _expressible_, except perhaps cloaked behind polysyllabic obfuscations like "assigned sex at birth" (as if the doctors made a _mistake_!).
> The point being illustrated here is that if it's socially unacceptable for people who want to talk about sex to say "That's not what I meant by _woman_ in this context _and you know it_", then people who would prefer not to acknowledge sex will always get the last word, not because they have superior arguments, but because the very terms of discourse have been [systematically gamed to conflate dissent with unkindness](/2018/Jan/dont-negotiate-with-terrorist-memeplexes/).
> If—like me—you're the kind of person who is not necessarily _happy_ about sexual dimorphism, you can always deliberately define your categories in order to minimize it: if there's a large sex difference in some observable measurement, just say you _don't care_ about predicting that particular measurement.
> Should such people be called women? Honestly, I lean towards _Yes_, but I can at least _see the argument_ of someone who preferred not to use the word that way.
> And, bluntly, the fact that _the smartest people I know_ tend to direct more of their effort towards redefining top-20 nouns than on biotechnology research, does not exactly inspire confidence or hope.
