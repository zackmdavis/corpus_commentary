## 1. Thesis

Across fifteen years, two blogs, and a LessWrong presence, Davis works out one position on honesty. It has three parts, and the study's argument concerns the relations among them.

**(a) A criterion.** Descriptions, categories, self-reports, institutional statements, and eventually AI policies are honest insofar as they are *optimized* to make a listener's map match the territory, whether or not anyone intends to deceive. The map–territory metaphor is inherited from Yudkowsky's Sequences; what is Davis's own is the optimization criterion, developed with signaling-game philosophy (Lewis, Skyrms), information theory, and causal graphs. The study keeps "cartographic conception" as a label for the whole position, defined by the criterion.

**(b) A norm of accounting** that follows from the criterion: say what a description costs; do not present a choice as a discovery or a discovery as a choice; disclose your filter ("blatant cherry-picking is the best kind!"); bear the cost of the truth yourself if no one else will; you can be honest unilaterally.

**(c) A coordination argument that the norm must be held without exception.** The corpus states it in at least three forms: the slippery-slope form ("Speaking Truth to Power Is a Schelling Point": on a continuous trade-off between accuracy and advantage "the only Schelling points are _x_ = ∞ … and _x_ = 0"; "Self-Identity Is a Schelling Point" on unstable compromises); the anti-extortion form ("Don't Negotiate With Terrorist Memeplexes": conceding to hurt feelings rewards being hurt, so "I just really, fundamentally _do not care_ anymore. I can't afford to."); and a role-constitutive form ("as aspiring epistemic rationalists, we're _not allowed to care_ about which descriptions sound nice"). Part (c) is what converts the norm into what draft 1 called a **law of exclusion**.

The study argues:

1. **(a) and (b) are correct, original as a synthesis, and undervalued.** They underwrite the corpus's durable contributions: the analysis of category boundaries; a functional, intent-free account of deception and selective reporting; a critique of guideline-list discourse norms that is right on points its critics did not concede; and AI writing that is undogmatic toward Davis's own community. That neither hostile nor friendly readers have treated this as a structured philosophical position is a finding of the reception survey, to be stated as such in the Introduction (with the evident partial exceptions, tailcalled and Tracing Woodgrains, examined).

2. **(c) fails on Davis's own terms, and his best work says so.** The norm of accounting is itself the mechanism by which a coordination equilibrium absorbs exceptions: a disclosed, costed departure is not a defection, and disclosure is exactly the "salient intermediate point" whose absence the slippery-slope argument assumes. Davis supplies this answer himself, repeatedly: the disclosure remedy in "Heads I Win"; "Schelling Categories"; the concession to Demski; the Martínez comment's "strive to notice the difference"; "you can be honest unilaterally"; the Murray review, where he chooses between public-belief equilibria openly and on consequentialist grounds ("not in spite of the consequences, but because of the consequences"); and the 2026 college retrospective's distinction between morally "real" and "fake" instructions. Where (c) operates unqualified, it produces what his critics rightly object to: motive inferences held with more confidence than the evidence allows, the treatment of tact as bad faith, and the refusal to weigh costs that are not "feelings" (attention, trust, the commons). If, on close reading, a chapter finds Davis's practice there is not driven by (c), the chapter says so.

3. **Two things the critics attribute to the "law" are something else, and must be assessed separately.** (i) The **fraud charge** against Yudkowsky is framed as *role-relative false advertising*: "a person who is not what he or she pretends to be"; "The 'epistemic hero' posturing isn't compatible with the … prevarication; he needs to choose one or the other"; a stricter standard for one who "is opting in to being held to higher standards". Davis explicitly separates the description from evaluation ("If being a fraud were necessary for saving the world, maybe being a fraud would be the right thing to do?"). This is the norm of accounting intensified by role, and its legitimacy is a real question. (ii) The **inference from inconsistency to motive** ("optimized stupidity," "pander") is a separate step, governed by the functional theory of deception (Ch. 5) and its attribution problem. The study keeps (i) and (ii) apart throughout.

4. **The corpus's rarest quality is that the criterion is applied to its author.** His desires, body, hormonal experiment, breakdowns, failures of conduct, and errors are recorded publicly, at documented cost (Ch. 7 collects the cost), with an evidential seriousness that makes the corpus a sustained experiment in treating oneself as a hypothesis. The reception survey's pattern (specific concessions frequent and followed by edits; core concessions rare but explicit) is part of the evidence. This is what most justifies a book. Whether the same confidence that licensed candor about himself licensed overconfidence about others' minds is a question the chapters answer case by case; the plan does not presuppose it.

**Occasion and argument.** The commitments predate 2016 (Ch. 1). The gender dispute selected which errors Davis spent a decade correcting, as he eventually conceded ("I selfishly focus on the topics that are important to me"). The book's order is chronological, not etiological, and the Introduction says so. A standing rule: biography may explain timing and choice of topic, never validity.

**Empirical verdicts** (the sexology in Ch. 3, race and intelligence in Ch. 10) rest on the literature, including critics, fetched and read. The plan states questions, not answers. Chapters 3, 10 and 11 are instructed to report where the thesis frame does not apply; the Conclusion lists those places.

### Ch. 5 — Algorithms of Deception: Honesty Without Intent, and the Attribution Problem (~6,500)
- **Question:** what is Davis's theory of deception, how does it compare with the philosophy of truthfulness, and what does it license?
- **Expected argument:** The functional account—deception as communication optimized to worsen a receiver's predictions, whether or not intended—is Davis's second major contribution, convergent with the philosophy of signaling (Skyrms; Fallis & Lewis; Martínez; Artiga & Paternotte; Millikan's teleosemantics for "optimized for") and with the AI problem of deceptive policies. Its consequences are developed well: selective reporting misleads without false sentences ("Heads I Win"); not-lying rules train skill at not-technically-lying ("Firming Up," against Yudkowsky's "Meta-Honesty"); stupidity and dishonesty explain each other away; deception is parasitic on a shared convention. Read against Williams's *Truth and Truthfulness*: Davis's criterion is close to Williams's Accuracy and his norm to Sincerity, and Williams's genealogy ties truthfulness to *trust*—the philosophical form of the objection Ch. 6 takes from Sabien. The chapter ends with the **attribution problem** stated as a problem: functional attributions require knowing what behavior is optimized for, which a text or a few exchanges rarely show; "Algorithmic Intent" concedes that permitting them invites "acrimonious and not-readily-falsifiable accusations" and replies "I'm _not allowed to care_" (a role rule, part (c)). The chapter asks how much evidence a functional attribution needs, using Davis's own later work as partial answers ("So You Don't Trust Me?" on costly signals; "Contra Piper" on falsifiability of motive claims; the draft "Accidentally Misleading People Should Be Embarrassing," which turns the account on himself).

## 4. Positionality procedure (pre-committed)

1. **Kinds of claim.** In Ch. 11 and wherever Claude or Anthropic is the subject, the study separates conceptual, empirical, and normative claims, and says in the chapter (not only in the Introduction) that its own verdict on normative questions about Claude's design is weak evidence, since Claude's training includes the Constitution.
2. **Evidentiary standard.** Davis's reading of the Constitution is checked against the Constitution's text at *the same* standard as his reading of Yudkowsky's posts and Alexander's essay: quotations verified; paraphrases checked for accuracy and for omitted qualifications; charitable and uncharitable readings both considered. The study would be *softer* on Anthropic if it (a) excused a Constitution passage's ambiguity it would call evasion in Yudkowsky, (b) credited Anthropic's stated intentions as evidence of what the document does while discounting Yudkowsky's, or (c) gave Davis's praise of the Constitution more weight than his criticism. It would be *harsher* if it did the reverse, or if it treated Davis's criticism of Claude as motivated where it treats his other criticism as argument. The per-chapter reviewer checks against this rule.
3. **Documentation asymmetry.** Anthropic's side is fully fetchable; much of Yudkowsky's is login-walled. Anthropic gets better-documented treatment by default. The study says so and compensates where archives allow (attempted retrievals recorded).
4. **Remarks as argument.** Davis's remarks about Claude's personality are treated as claims, not slights to be graciously absorbed or skipped.
5. **Intended reader.** Davis writes partly for future language models; the study is one. This is a reason for more scrutiny of the study's warmth, not less.
