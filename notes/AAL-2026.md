### Contra Pace on When to Apologize
- Path: `AAL_content/2026/contra-pace-on-when-to-apologize.md` · 2026-06-18 · philosophy · 1795 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2026/Jun/contra-pace-on-when-to-apologize/
- Summary: *BoJack Horseman* epigraph ("I don't forgive you"). Against Ben Pace's "Financial Ledger Theory of Apologies" (take costs "on one's own ledger" without changing behavior). - Themes: apology, forgiveness, ben-pace, ledger-metaphor, policy-update, honesty
- Quotes:

> When someone wrongs me, it seems like the _smallest_ ask I could reasonably make in exchange for my forgiveness is that they not do that again
> Debtors don't get to unilaterally decide how much debt to write in their ledger.
> It always _looks_ better to write down a larger number, but the difference between a large number backed by assets and a large number backed by the desire to write down a large number is the difference between generosity and fraud.

- Path: `AAL_content/2026/so-you-dont-trust-me.md` · 2026-08-26 · philosophy (honesty) · 1311 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2026/Aug/so-you-dont-trust-me/
- Summary: Builds on the *Atlas Shrugged* line (Cherryl) that "honest people were never touchy about the matter of being trusted." To be trusted you must be *known* trustworthy, which requires "saying something that a dishonest person couldn't just as easily say" — a separating equilibrium. Worked example: cat insurance (healthy vs. sick cats; adverse selection; separating vs. pooling equilibria; pooling makes healthy cats bear others' risk). Dagny/Ellis Wyatt: an honest businessperson trapped in a pooling equilibrium with corrupt ones can't plead innocence; non-defensiveness slightly softens distrust. "the hidden Bayesian structure of the universe is not kind. Those who try to wriggle out of paying the price should understand what signal they're sending."
- Themes: honesty, signaling, separating-equilibrium, adverse-selection, ayn-rand, trust
- Xrefs: AAL 2021 "Communication Requires Common Interests or Differential Signal Costs"; AAL 2023 "Justice, Cherryl."; AAL 2026 "Contra Piper."
- Quotes:
> A solution, if there is one, has to take the form of [saying something that a dishonest person couldn't just as easily say](/blog/2021/Mar/communication-requires-common-interests-or-differential-signal-costs/).
> And that's why honest people are never touchy about the matter of being trusted. They know that in order to distinguish themselves from dishonest people, they have to pay a price that's too expensive for dishonest people to pay.

### Contra Piper on When Conversation Is Possible
- Path: `AAL_content/2026/contra-piper-on-when-conversation-is-possible.md` · 2026-09-07 · philosophy (honesty) · 1411 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2026/Sep/contra-piper-on-when-conversation-is-possible/
- Summary: Replies to a Kelsey Piper tweet (to Richard Ngo) that declaring someone self-deceived "inherently makes conversation with them nearly impossible." Davis "categorically reject[s]" this. Accusations of self-deception aren't unfalsifiable: beliefs/motives aren't epiphenomenal (the thirsty-animal-at-the-dried-stream example); someone can argue your stated motives are inconsistent with your observable behavior while staying agnostic about your qualia. The salon/girlfriend's-bakery example (documented comparison shopping as evidence against favoritism) gives "something to talk about." Why would someone think it's impossible? A self-interested reason: feeling guilty and wanting to shut down conversations that risk exposure — only talk to people who already trust you. So Piper's advice reads "as a negotiating threat: don't criticize us in this way, or we'll walk." "But if you think I'm kidding myself about that, then I'm happy to talk about it."
- Themes: self-deception, kelsey-piper, richard-ngo, conversation, negotiating-threats, assume-bad-faith, honesty
- Xrefs: AAL 2023 "Assume Bad Faith," "Plea Bargaining"; AAL 2026 "So You Don't Trust Me"; UUT 2025 Piper interview. **Reception:** Piper/Ngo Twitter exchange (login-walled; archives?).
- Quotes:
> I categorically reject the idea that rejecting someone's understanding of their beliefs and motives inherently makes conversation with them nearly impossible.
> In light of these observations, I think Piper's apparently well-meaning advice is more naturally understood as a negotiating threat: don't criticize us in this way, or we'll walk.
> But if you think I'm kidding myself about that, then I'm happy to talk about it.

- Summary: Opens with unguarded contempt for self-styled truthseekers who "block at the drop of a hat" and derail into "tone" litigation — "protecting their egos from signal that would hurt their feelings." He tests whether the analogy should make him merciful to "listening disorders" and concludes tentatively not — information aversion is "a major if not the primary obstacle to human rationality," whereas his food aversion is low-cost and self-regarding — but resolves to be "more clear-eyed and empathetic about how much pain I'm dealing."

### Dance, Dance
- Path: `AAL_content/2026/dance-dance.md` · 2026-10-05 · psychology (dance) · 1346 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2026/Oct/dance-dance/
- Summary: In his youth Davis "affected a standoffish attitude on the topic of _games_," writing a chess engine (Leafline) and a Zendo solver (Mezzanine) rather than learning to play; now he's learned chess properly (2024). His current fixation is the arrow-stomping game (DDR and variants). Its interest is as a *robotics/Moravec's-paradox* problem: the game-as-timers and the optimal-agent-as-timers coincide, so it's only hard for an embodied agent converting visual cues to a motor plan. He uses it as an extended metaphor for how LLMs/gradient methods learn: dense reward "judgments" (Marvelous/Perfect/Great/Good/Miss; Early/Late as gradient info) shape a policy; "chunking" patterns; crossovers emerge without being practiced or named, describable "_only in retrospect_." Contrast with chess accounts stuck at flat ratings, and his failed 2024 gymnastics attempt — where the reward signal wasn't dense enough. Ties to the "strange inversion of reasoning" and the AI "end-time": legible symbolic reasoning is the *output* of a process that isn't itself shaped like symbolic reasoning. "The steps we were already taking implied the seeds of the revolution."
- Themes: games, reinforcement-learning, moravec's-paradox, skill-acquisition, llms, embodiment, ai-end-time, biography
- Xrefs: AAL 2019 "Minimax Search and the Structure of Cognition"; AAL 2024 "'Deep Learning' Is Function Approximation"; AAL 2026 "College" (learned chess 2024).
- Quotes:
> The games that endure are sports, which appear cognitively trivial only because the form of cognition demanded is more ancient than language, with the effect that we can't talk about it and the pretraining set (for all the vastness of webtext) doesn't cover it.
> Competences emerge as solutions determined by the training signal.
> The alternative to incrementally accreting complex skills from local rewards isn't designing them from clean principles—but not learning them at all.
> Apprehension of this [strange inversion of reasoning](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0904433106) almost makes the present end-time seem like [less of a grotesque surprise](https://www.readthesequences.com/Think-Like-Reality).
