### On the Contrary, Steelmanning Is Normal; ITT-Passing Is Niche
- Path: `AAL_content/2024/on-the-contrary-steelmanning-is-normal-itt-passing-is-niche.md` · 2024-01-09 · philosophy (discourse) · 1338 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2024/Jan/on-the-contrary-steelmanning-is-normal-itt-passing-is-niche/
- Summary: LW-first. Replies to Rob Bensinger ("ITT-passing and civility are good; 'charity' is bad; steelmanning is niche"). Davis reverses it: the Ideological Turing Test models others' models rather than reality ("I couldn't pass an ITT for advocates of Islam or extrasensory perception … my inability to pass an ITT for Islam or ESP seems ... basically fine"), and as a "selfish seeker of truth" he neither wants to pass nor demands it; he asks for it only when he suspects bad faith. Steelmanning falls out of selfish argument-improvement (programmer analogy: accepting suggestions with your own changes). "Having to explicitly drag out the ITT is a sign of sickness, not a sign of health." Ends with Steven Kaas's 2008 quote "fight the most horrible thing that can be constructed from its corpse."
- Themes: discourse-norms, itt, steelmanning, bensinger, selfish-truth-seeking
- Quotes:
> I think Bensinger has it backwards: the Ideological Turing Test is a useful niche skill, but it doesn't belong on a list of things to organize a discussion around, whereas something like steelmanning naturally falls out of object-level learning.
> Crucially, _the same is true in the "inbound" direction_. I don't expect people to be able to pass my ITT before criticizing my ideas. That would make it harder for people to inform me about flaws in my ideas!
> Having to explicitly drag out the ITT is a sign of sickness, not a sign of health.

Simplicia's evidence: modular-arithmetic interpretability (two algorithms, clock/midpoint circles); word2vec/FastText converging in relative representations; adversarial examples as useful features; robustness to massive label noise; counting arguments would also predict overfitting. Doomimir's position: deception is convergent (ant baits); "endpoints are easier to predict than intermediate trajectories"; things optimized to look good diverge off-distribution; retargetable search needs a goal slot; playing the training game; "very, very confident." "The model _is_ the model." Ends on a cliffhanger (the mailman; "Intermission").
- Themes: ai-alignment, doomimir-simplicia, llms, generalization, deceptive-alignment, claude-criticism, constitutional-ai, interpretability
- Xrefs: AAL 2023 first dialogue; AAL 2024 "The Standard Analogy" (sequel); 2026 constitution posts. **Positionality:** criticizes Claude/Anthropic (through a character's voice).
- Quotes:
> **Simplicia**: Why doesn't the "predicting barroom conversation doesn't make you drunk" analogy falsely imply "predicting the answers to modular arithmetic problems doesn't mean you implement modular arithmetic"?
> **Doomimir**: [_Mis_-generalization?](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/dkjwSLfvKwpaQSuWo/misgeneralization-as-a-misnomer) The goals _you_ wanted [aren't a property of the training data itself](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PoDAyQMWEXBBBEJ5P/magical-categories). The danger comes from _correct_ generalization implying something you don't want.
> as I mentioned the other day, [Anthropic Claude's preachy, condescending personality](https://nostalgebraist.tumblr.com/post/728556535745232896/claude-is-insufferable) already gives me the creeps.
> It makes sense that a language model agent trained to conform to principles [chosen by a committee at a California public benefit corporation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claudes-constitution) would act like _that_.
> Rather, it sounds like you don't think training can shape motivations—"inner" motivations—at all.
> **Simplicia**: That makes sense to me if you posit a preëxisting consequentialist reasoner being slotted into a contemporary ML training setup and trying to minimize loss. But that's not what's going on? Language models aren't an agent that _has_ a model. The model _is_ the model.
> I'm very, very confident that any AI produced via anything remotely like the current paradigm is not going to end up wanting what we want, even if it's harder to say exactly when it will go off the rails or what it will want instead.

### "Deep Learning" Is Function Approximation
- Path: `AAL_content/2024/deep-learning-is-function-approximation.md` · 2024-03-21 · computing · 2987 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2024/Mar/deep-learning-is-function-approximation/
- Summary: (LW cross-post likely.) A deadpan "taboo your words" register: "multi-layer parameterized graphical function approximators," "success-antecedent computation boosting," "some authors call ... for some reason." Double descent; "What did _you_ think it was doing?" DQN as swapping a Q-table for a function approximator. Critiques the "List of Lethalities" (Yudkowsky; unnamed, "the author keeps using the term 'AGI' without defining it"): an outer loss doesn't create an inner representation, but "why would you want that?"; "the model is the dataset"; "Mean squared error _of what?_" Threat model: agents built deliberately with function-approximator components (AlphaGo/MCTS) rather than inner agents; an action-to-money approximator would steer toward fraud, i.e. "approximating the wrong function." Jack Gallagher quote ("wheels must be made of little cars"). Ends humble. Bibliography lists "Bardo, Richard S., and Andrew G. Sutton. 2024. _Reinforcement Learning_" (the text is Sutton & Barto; possibly a deliberate garble in keeping with the "some authors" joke — note for checking).
- Themes: deep-learning, function-approximation, taboo-words, list-of-lethalities-critique, threat-models, rl, alphago
- Quotes:
> (Some authors call these ["deep neural networks"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning) for some reason, but [I like my name better](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/WBdvyyHLdxZSAMmoz/taboo-your-words).)
> Piecewise-linear functions are still piecewise-linear functions even when there are a lot of pieces. What did _you_ think it was doing?
> But "training" is just a [suggestive name](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yxWbbe9XcgLFCrwiL/dreams-of-ai-alignment-the-danger-of-suggestive-names).
> It's just not meaningful to talk about what a loss function implies, independently of the training data. (Mean squared error _of what?_ Negative log likelihood _of what?_ Finish the sentence!)
> If I had to guess, I'd imagine someone deliberately building an agent using function approximators as a critical component, rather than your function approximator secretly having an agent inside of it.
> The problem is that you're approximating the wrong function and [get what you measure](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/HBxe6wdjxK239zajf/what-failure-looks-like#Part_I__You_get_what_you_measure).
> Just a conviction that, whatever challenges confront us in the future, we'll be a better position to face them by understanding the problem in as much detail as possible.

### Ironing Out the Squiggles

- Summary: LW-first. Explainer on adversarial examples: gradient attacks; cross-model transfer; Ilyas et al. on non-robust but useful features (a model trained on adversarial examples generalizes); Gilmer et al.'s "Adversarial Spheres" (on-distribution adversaries in high dimensions). Adversarial training (Madry et al., PGD min–max): robust MNIST perturbations become human-meaningful (images in `AAL_content/images/`, view). Probably."
- Themes: adversarial-examples, robustness, alignment-cruxes, yudkowsky-vs-christiano, perception, ml-explainer
- Quotes:
> On this view, adversarial examples arise from gradient descent being "too smart", not "too dumb": the program is fine; if the test suite didn't imply the behavior we wanted, that's our problem.
> The robustified models are close enough to seeing the same animals we are that adversarial attacks against them are also attacks against us
> > Eliezer expects weird squiggles from gradient descent
> To my inexpert eyes, the Gaziv _et al._ "perceptual wormholes" result does seem like a clue that "ironing out the squiggles" may prove to be feasible after all

### The Standard Analogy

Doomimir's "empirical evidence" is the evolution analogy; Simplicia wanted evidence "from this century." On Sutton's bitter lesson ("The autogenocidal maniac Richard Sutton," in Doomimir's voice), Simplicia offers resnets as an example of real science; Doomimir: "_That's_ your example?" Simplicia's possible crux: how narrow the target is. Doomimir: "Your mother named you well."

### Comment on "Death and the Gorgon"
- Path: `AAL_content/2024/comment-on-death-and-the-gorgon.md` · 2024-12-31 · arts & culture (fiction review) · 2937 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2024/Dec/comment-on-death-and-the-gorgon/
- Summary: LW-first. Review of Greg Egan's "Death and the Gorgon" (*Asimov's*, Jan/Feb 2024): a cryonics-lottery murder scheme run by "Optimized Giving," a satire of longtermist EA. "Reality and Greg Egan are starting to meet in the middle." Davis concedes membership in the satirized subculture while disclaiming loyalty; "the hubris, the deception, and the waste of money" resonates with his memoir; EA "ate my beloved rationalism"; FTX parallel. A footnote calls criticizing the movement "_boring_ ... forget about these losers already." Faults the satire for dismissal rather than counterarguments (simulation argument, grabby aliens look "reasonable to me"), and finds it odd that the author of *Diaspora* treats powerful-AI concern as the cult's characteristic belief (cites the CAIS extinction statement). Egan's tweet about "early, funny ones" that "predate the cult." Davis: "personally, _I_ like Egan's later work"; "a discerning loyalty to whatever was _good_."
- Themes: greg-egan, fiction-review, effective-altruism-critique, ftx, ai-risk-credibility, subculture-membership, discerning-loyalty
- Quotes:
> So, cards on the table: this story spends a lot of wordcount satirizing a subculture that, unfortunately, I can't credibly claim not to be a part of.
> I don't want to be defensive, because I'm _not_ loyal to the subculture, its leaders, or its institutions.
> I keep telling myself that one of these years I'm going to have healed enough trauma to forget about these losers already and just read ArXiv papers.
> Accepting the premise that institutional longtermist EA deserves condemnation for being goofy and a fraud: in condemning them, why single out as the characteristic belief of this despicable group, the idea that future AI could be really powerful?
> an ethos that doesn't endorse blind loyalty to every author or group you learned something from, but a discerning loyalty to whatever was _good_ in what the author or group saw in our shared universe.
