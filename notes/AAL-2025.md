### Discontinuous Linear Functions?!
- Path: `AAL_content/2025/discontinuous-linear-functions.md` · 2025-06-05 · mathematics (analysis) · 684 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2025/Jun/discontinuous-linear-functions/
- Summary: The derivative operator on C¹([a,b]) under the uniform norm is linear but discontinuous (sin(kx)/k → 0 while cos(kx) doesn't converge); in finite dimensions eigenvalues bound the stretch, in infinite dimensions they don't. Thanks Jeffrey Liang and Gurkenglas for corrections (an earlier version erred about normed spaces; see next entry).
- Themes: mathematics, analysis, self-correction
- Quotes:
> Actually, it's not crazy. It's just that all the discontinuous linear functions live in infinite-dimensional spaces.

### Critic Contributions Are Logically Irrelevant
- Path: `AAL_content/2025/critic-contributions-are-logically-irrelevant.md` · 2025-07-14 · philosophy (epistemology) · 1910 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2025/Jul/critic-contributions-are-logically-irrelevant/
- Summary: LW-first. Against Oliver Habryka's coach-who-doesn't-play and Duncan Sabien's builders-vs-critics metaphors: a comment's value lies in its text, not its author ("screens off"); Davis uses his own Discontinuous Linear Functions error, corrected by a comment, as the example ("It doesn't matter whether they're _human_"). Author-based priors matter under uncertainty, but post-writing isn't evidence about comment quality the way playing is for coaching. Conclusion: if you disapprove of commenters who don't write posts, "_you care about something other than correctness_" (status allocation, ad revenue).
- Themes: discourse-norms, lesswrong-moderation, habryka, sabien, criticism, screening-off
- Xrefs: LW 2025–26 Said Achmiz posts (in LW_content); AAL 2023 discourse cluster.
- Quotes:
> I find this stance deeply puzzling. In general, the value of a critical blog comment is in potentially alerting readers to an error, omission, or other shortcoming of the post.
> That was wrong of me. The thing I wrote was wrong.
> It doesn't matter whether they're _human_. If a large language model had written the same comment, it would be the same comment.
> The inference also works in the other direction: if you disapprove of commenters who don't write posts, that implies that _you care about something other than correctness_.

### The Relationship Between Social Punishment and Shared Maps
- Path: `AAL_content/2025/the-relationship-between-social-punishment-and-shared-maps.md` · 2025-10-08 · social science · 1317 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2025/Oct/the-relationship-between-social-punishment-and-shared-maps/
- Summary: Social punishment is implemented through reputation, i.e. negative-valence beliefs on shared maps; unlike rocks, "_Beliefs can be true or false._" Attempts to limit social punishment without costing map accuracy are "mostly an illusion": Alice publishing true evidence that Mallory lied functions as punishment; "judgments supervene on facts and values"; "neutral" tone may succeed only by obfuscation. At best, omit your own policy response: "Personally, I buy it, but I don't think Bob, Carol, or Dave do."
- Themes: reputation, social-punishment, shared-maps, criticism-of-persons, honesty
- Xrefs: UUT 2023 "A Hill of Validity" (reputational damage to Yudkowsky); AAL 2023 "Assume Bad Faith."
- Quotes:
> We notice a critical difference between social punishments and physical punishments. _Beliefs can be true or false._
> It's mostly an illusion.
> But judgments [supervene](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/supervenience/) on facts and values. If lying is bad, and Mallory lied, it logically follows that Mallory did a bad thing.
> Is that enough to make the clinical description not a "social punishment"? Personally, I buy it, but I don't think Bob, Carol, or Dave do.

### "Yes, and—" Requires the Possibility of "No, Because—"
- Path: `AAL_content/2025/yes-and-requires-the-possibility-of-no-because.md` · 2025-10-09 · social science (discourse) · 1086 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2025/Oct/yes-and-requires-the-possibility-of-no-because/
- Summary: Garrabrant's principle in information-theoretic terms (zero entropy if the answer is always Yes). Improv's "Yes, and" and the ban on blocking (Agent Stone / Dr. Skull) as an analogy for collaborative truthseeking norms: polite partial disagreement is part of the "offer," while wholesale negation is blocking. Under those norms, falsehood and confusion accumulate; "There's a reason improv is almost synonymous with improv _comedy_." "if you're committed to validating your interlocutor's contribution to the scene before providing your own, you're not really talking to _each other_."
- Themes: discourse-norms, collaborative-truth-seeking, improv, information-theory
- Quotes:
> What happens when someone says something wrong or confusing or unclear? If their interlocutor prioritizes correctness and clarity, the natural behavior is to say, "No, that's wrong, because ..." or "No, I didn't understand that"—and not only that, but _to maintain that "No" until clarity is forthcoming_. That's blocking.
> There's a reason improv is almost synonymous with improv _comedy_.
