### Algorithmic Intent: A Hansonian Generalized Anti-Zombie Principle
- Path: `AAL_content/2020/algorithmic-intent-a-hansonian-generalized-anti-zombie-principle.md` · 2020-07-13 · philosophy (philosophy of language) · 3668 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2020/Jul/algorithmic-intent-a-hansonian-generalized-anti-zombie-principle/
- Summary: LW-first. By analogy with Yudkowsky's GAZP, posit "intent" as causally upstream of optimized behavior: "You can't have a zombie that _just happens_ to systematically select actions that result in outcomes that rank high with respect to a recognizable preference ordering _for no reason_." Sorting example: computers really sort; intent epiphenomenal w.r.t. sorting but not to the universe; robustness to perturbation (Alex Flint). Hidden motives (Hanson & Simler: primate grooming; Ziz's father/NVC example; Caravelle's game loophole; his own convenient mishearing while baking). Proposes "algorithmic" as disambiguating adjective (algorithmic intent, honesty, deception, fraud), via Jessica Taylor's metaphorical-extension concept; fireflies "straight-up" deceptive; GAN learning; AI-written fraud. Concedes the concern that permitting unconscious-motive attributions invites acrimonious accusations may be "_correct_," but "as an aspiring epistemic rationalist, I'm _not allowed to care_."
- Themes: algorithmic-intent, hidden-motives, robin-hanson, self-deception, deception, philosophy-of-mind, ziz, jessica-taylor, ai-and-intent
- Xrefs: AAL 2013 "The History of the Universe" (intentional stance); AAL 2019 "Maybe Lying Doesn't Exist"; AAL 2023 "Assume Bad Faith".
- Quotes:
> The Hansonian Generalized Anti-Zombie Principle calls for us to posit "intent" as causally upstream of optimized behavior (even if the causal link might be complicated and we might be wrong about the details of what _intent_ is). You can't have a zombie that _just happens_ to systematically select actions that result in outcomes that rank high with respect to a recognizable preference ordering _for no reason_.
> In contrast, the father is likely to "lose control" and make angry threats precisely _when_ peaceful behavior _isn't getting him what he wants_.
> But I claim that broad "algorithmic" senses of "mental" words like _intent_ often are more relevant and useful for making sense of the world than the original, narrower definitions that were invented by humans in the context of dealing with other humans, because the universe _in fact_ does not revolve around humans.
> It seems plausible to me that this concern is _correct_: in a human community of any appreciable size, if you let people [question the stories we tell about ourselves](http://unremediatedgender.space/2016/Sep/psychology-is-about-invalidating-peoples-identities/), you _are_ going to get acrimonious and not-readily-falsifiable accusations of bad intent.
> Unfortunately, as an aspiring epistemic rationalist, [I'm _not allowed to care_](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/bSmgPNS6MTJsunTzS/maybe-lying-doesn-t-exist#Appeals_to_Consequences_Are_Invalid) whether some descriptions might be socially harmful for a human community to adopt; I'm _only_ allowed to care about what descriptions [shorten the length of the message](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/f4txACqDWithRi7hs/occam-s-razor) needed to describe my observations.

### Maybe Lying Can't Exist?!
- Path: `AAL_content/2020/maybe-lying-cant-exist.md` · 2020-08-22 · philosophy (honesty) · 1633 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2020/Aug/maybe-lying-cant-exist/
- Summary: LW-first. In sender–receiver games meaning = informational effect, so how can a signal lie? First attempt: deception = signals that make receivers update less accurately while benefiting the sender (mechanistically). Firefly *P. rey*/*P. redator* example; after counteradaptation, the mimic signal is "priced in" (Fallis & Lewis bite the bullet; *Moral Mazes* "if everyone knows ... is a lie really a lie?"). Davis prefers understanding laws over tidy definitions (O'Connor). Skyrms & Barrett: propositional content defined in the common-interest subgame; deception is "ontologically parasitic" like holes; "a sufficiently deceptive map ... collapses into noise and dust."
- Themes: deception, signaling-games, skyrms, propositional-content, philosophy-of-language
- Quotes:
> Thus, we see that deception is ["ontologically parasitic" in sense that holes are](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/holes/#Theo). You can't have a hole without some material for it to be a hole _in_; you can't have a lie without some shared map for it to be a lie _in_. And a sufficiently deceptive map, like a sufficiently holey material, collapses into noise and dust.
> Personally, I don't care much about having tidy formal definitions of English words; I want to understand the general [_laws_ governing](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eY45uCCX7DdwJ4Jha/no-one-can-exempt-you-from-rationality-s-laws) the construction and perversion of shared maps, even if a detailed understanding requires revising or splitting some of our intuitive concepts.

### Message Length
- Path: `AAL_content/2020/message-length.md` · 2020-10-19 · philosophy (information theory, Rust) · 3157 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2020/Oct/message-length/
- Summary: LW-first (139 score). Second-person narrative inferring structure in a bitstream: coin vs. Markov chains of increasing order; maximum likelihood in Rust; log loss monotonically improves with order (overfitting; "gerrymandered" target); log loss as code length; theories also cost bits; MDL with 32·2ⁿ complexity penalty selects third order on 10,000 bits. "That's the power packed by parsimony—the sublime simplicity of _Science_."
- Themes: minimum-description-length, occams-razor, overfitting, information-theory, programming
- Xrefs: Applies to category boundaries: "gerrymandered" fits (cf. AAL 2021 "Unnatural Categories…").
- Quotes:
> Higher values of _n_ [are like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfitting) a ... thinner paintbrush?—or a squigglier, more "gerrymandered" painting of a target.
> _Once you stated the theory_. A theory implies an optimally efficient coding by which further bits can whittle down the space of possibilities to the data that actually happened. More complicated or unlikely data requires more bits just to _specify_—to single out that one outcome amongst the vastness of equally remote alternatives. But [_the same thing goes for theories_](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nj8JKFoLSMEmD3RGp/how-much-evidence-does-it-take).
