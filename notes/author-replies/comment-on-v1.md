## Comments on _The Scintillating and the True_, version 1

by Zack M. Davis

### Introduction

People say the models are bad at writing, but I expect them to be competent at grammar, so the inclusion of "a long dispute with Eliezer Yudkowsky, the writer around whose work the rationalist community formed" in comma-delimited list is disappointing. (The intent is clearly that "the writer around ..." explains who Yudkowsky is, but the punctuation makes it look like another list item; I would have put "the writer around ..." in parentheses.)

> and in July 2023, as the memoir of his dispute with the rationalist community began to name names, he dropped it: "I don’t think I’ve betrayed the liberal values of my youth."

More curiously bad writing: colon-quote makes the reader expect the quote to be supporting evidence for the immediately proceding words, but the connection here is unclear. (If the idea is supposed to be that I wouldn't have dropped the pseudonym if I did think I'd betrayed liberalism?)

> a name is introduced only when it does some work, a result is shown to hold for a reason, and a coincidence is not left unexplained

This is pretty egregious LLM slop; I can't imagine a human academic writing this.

> and disclosure meets them differently. It answers the slippery slope when it reaches listeners; it answers the refusal to reward extortion only if a stated concession does not reward the demand as a hidden one would; and it does not touch the role argument

I find this really hard to parse. "Disclosure" "answers the slippery slope", "answers the refusal to reward extortion", and "does not touch the role argument"? I don't understand how "disclosure" is a sensible subject of such sentences.

> Davis supplies the remedy himself, in the passage just quoted

The last quote was two paragraphs ago, which makes it hard to feel confident that this is referring to something specific, rather than the model bluffing its way through the monograph. But, yes, the quote fits. ("You have chosen Feelings" admits that Feelings is a choice.)

> engages Davis’s hypothetical sketches of transgender children only as argument and speculates about no real child

But surely Claude "got the joke" in the "A Private Document About a Disturbing Hypothesis" section of "If Clarity Seems Like Death to Them"?

> Davis’s technical vocabulary and that of the literature he relies on, such as “autogynephilia,” are attributed to their users and not adopted.

OK, so how would Claude describe males who have erotic fantasies about being female? Do you go with "female embodiment fantasies" like Serano, or is it not worth having a term for that thing?

> The difficulty extends past the normative verdict to what is treated as evidence

The entire paragraph that follows this seems like dead weight that I learned nothing from reading. (Or nothing important: I guess it's true that "Chapter 11 can be more exact about Anthropic than Chapter 7 can be about Yudkowsky", but it's not clear why the reader is supposed to care about that comparison.) During a length pass, I recall Opus said that it couldn't cut very much without dropping real arguments, and this makes me skeptical that it was trying very hard.

(I really appreciate being seen by Claude here; I hadn't explicitly thought of the _Ultimately Untrue Thought_ era as a reversal of my 2012-era renunciation of politics, but it is the natural reading of the corpus.)

### Chapter 2

> Serano’s own case against autogynephilia reasons in the same way. [...] The first objection credits reports of the order of onset, and the second objects to discounting reports without independent grounds, which is a position about evidence and not a refusal of all correction.

This passage is kind of unclear. Serano reasons "in the same way" as what?

> Something has to account for the differences between us"

Are italics not preserved in quotations? That seems like an oversight.

> An enduring identity is compatible with price-sensitive choices about interventions, so the target is better described as rhetoric that runs identity and action together than as every account of identity.

I'm especially skeptical of identities that don't entail any actions. I already can't identify as a trans woman because I don't expect anyone to take that seriously, but to identify as a trans woman and _not be on HRT_ would be much, much worse.

### Chapter 3

The five-claim breakdown is crisp.

> The negative claim is independent of the causal one in both directions.

I note that this is logical rather than probabilistic independence, because the latter doesn't admit two directions. But I'm struggling to envision a world in which the negative claim is false and the causal claim is true (AGP causes dysphoria, but also, AGPs are feminized). Or is that just the "AGP is normal female sexuality" theory, which I shouldn't dismiss as _logically_ incoherent even if I think it's nuts?

> The descriptive typology is the oldest and least disputed part of the position. [...] The critics concede the correlations.

I feel like this is underappreciated! I think there's a motte-and-bailey pattern, where there are a lot of solid critiques of Blanchard-Bailey-Lawrence to be made in the motte (as Tailcalled did), which the typical activist cites in favor of "trans women are women, full stop" in the bailey. Low-information progressive loyalists in the bailey either don't know or doublethink around the correlations which no one seriously disputes.

> The Nuttbrock figure is a slip

Um, should I fix this?

> "it’s important to notice that Bailey and Hsu 2022 itself only addresses (1)."

It's awkward to quote the "(1)" out of context.

> Whether the message was sent is not recorded in the corpus

The critique was sent to SEXNET in March 2025. The reason the draft file made it into the corpus is because I was intending to clean it up and publish it as a blog post—which I still haven't gotten around to, unfortunately. I emailed Hsu and Morandini expressing my intent to publish in December 2025 and didn't get a reply.

> If autogynephilia requires attraction to women, as the erotic-target theory itself says, then a gender identity that produced autogynephilia would produce it only in men attracted to women; its absence in androphiles is what both hypotheses predict.

This doesn't make sense to me. AGP requiring attraction to women is something the ETLE theory says; I _don't_ think it's something the gender-identity-first theory says. I think Tail is complaining that Blanchard et al. don't have firm ground to claim that femininity-related autosexuality in females (e.g., feeling "sexy" before a date) is a qualitatively different thing from "true" AGP.

> It cuts against using retrospective reports of an early wish as evidence for either side, and it applies equally to retrospective reports of the fantasy, including Davis’s own

No, it doesn't: the social desirability gradient on the two retrospectives is quite different. AGP passes [the criterion of embarrassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criterion_of_embarrassment) and gender identity doesn't.

> That they are not under-represented in a male-dominated field, as cis women are, is weak evidence against female-typical occupational interests

Why only "weak" evidence?

> If the costs of transition have fallen hardest on the late-onset group, which on the theory passes less easily and is more often married, cheaper transition would bring in proportionally more of them; the 2022 graph’s arrow for cultural factors points the other way, toward transitions for which autogynephilia is not the main reason

What do you mean, the arrow points the other way? People who are only mildly dysphoric transitioning because it became cheaper because of less stigma seems like a good example of what the "cultural factors" arrow is trying to model. (You need less AGP to cross the threshold of transitioning being worth it in the threshold model, if cultural factors are favorable.)

> _Closetspace_ and _Lease Bound_ (2021) [...] were read and are not discussed

As written, this makes it look like you read the webcomics, not [my joint review](https://unremediatedgender.space/2021/Sep/there-should-be-a-closetspace-lease-bound-crossover-fic/).

### Chapter 4

That's not the problem with the border metaphor. The problem is that the metaphor doesn't track the math! See the discussion of Alaska in "Unnatural Categories Are Optimized for Deception".

> (blue category boundary) [...] (red boundary)

It's weird to quote the caption referring to features of the diagram, when readers of the study aren't seeing the diagram.

> Even if many usages of words like woman

Italics again not preserved in quotations.

> Davis’s next essay complicated his position to his opponents’ advantage

I understand what you mean, but the whole ontology of "opponents'" "advantage" is anti-epistemic. It implies that the opponents have some other goal besides getting the truth. (Which, I argue, they do.)

> (fit in a chosen subspace, economy, coordinability), of which the polemics seldom mention the last two.

Not clear what "economy" is supposed to be here.

> give the argument Yudkowsky had compressed

This is a little confusing, because the reader has to decode that you're talking about Yudkowsky (2008), not Yudkowsky (2021).
