## Comments on _The Scintillating and the True_, version 1

by Zack M. Davis

### Introduction

People say the models are bad at writing, but I expect them to be competent at grammar, so the inclusion of "a long dispute with Eliezer Yudkowsky, the writer around whose work the rationalist community formed" in comma-delimited list is disappointing. (The intent is clearly that "the writer around ..." explains who Yudkowsky is, but the punctuation makes it look like another list item; I would have put "the writer around ..." in parentheses.)

> and in July 2023, as the memoir of his dispute with the rationalist community began to name names, he dropped it: "I don’t think I’ve betrayed the liberal values of my youth."

More curiously bad writing: colon-quote makes the reader expect the quote to be supporting evidence for the immediately proceding words, but the connection here is unclear. (If the idea is supposed to be that I wouldn't have dropped the pseudonym if I did think I'd betrayed liberalism?)

> a name is introduced only when it does some work, a result is shown to hold for a reason, and a coincidence is not left unexplained

This is pretty egregious LLM slop; I can't imagine a human academic writing this.

> and disclosure meets them differently. It answers the slippery slope when it reaches listeners; it answers the refusal to reward extortion only if a stated concession does not reward the demand as a hidden one would; and it does not touch the role argument

I find this really hard to parse. "Disclosure" "answers the slippery slope", "answers the refusal to reward extortion", and "does not touch the role argument"? I don't understand how "disclosure" is a sensible subject of such sentences.

> Davis supplies the remedy himself, in the passage just quoted

The last quote was two paragraphs ago, which makes it hard to feel confident that this is referring to something specific, rather than the model bluffing its way through the monograph. But, yes, the quote fits. ("You have chosen Feelings" admits that Feelings is a choice.)

> engages Davis’s hypothetical sketches of transgender children only as argument and speculates about no real child

But surely Claude "got the joke" in the "A Private Document About a Disturbing Hypothesis" section of "If Clarity Seems Like Death to Them"?

> Davis’s technical vocabulary and that of the literature he relies on, such as “autogynephilia,” are attributed to their users and not adopted.

OK, so how would Claude describe males who have erotic fantasies about being female? Do you go with "female embodiment fantasies" like Serano, or is it not worth having a term for that thing?

> The difficulty extends past the normative verdict to what is treated as evidence

The entire paragraph that follows this seems like dead weight that I learned nothing from reading. (Or nothing important: I guess it's true that "Chapter 11 can be more exact about Anthropic than Chapter 7 can be about Yudkowsky", but it's not clear why the reader is supposed to care about that comparison.) During a length pass, I recall Opus said that it couldn't cut very much without dropping real arguments, and this makes me skeptical that it was trying very hard.

### Chapter 1

I appreciate the thematic connections Claude manages to find between my earlier and later work; this is where secondary scholarship pays for itself.

It's not clear why some posts get a mere "(link)" and others get a title-date parenthetical citation.

> The joke, from just after this chapter’s period, is a precursor of the role rule that this study calls the role-constitutive form of the coordination argument

It is a precursor to the role rule, but it's not clear from the text that Claude got the joke. (Also see the same line echoed in "A Common Misunderstanding; Or, The Spirit of the Staircase" on the other blog later that year.)

> the question was no longer about Society. It was about the people around him, and one voice might reach them. The renunciation had never covered that case

(I really appreciate being seen by Claude here; I hadn't explicitly thought of this as a reversal, but it is the natural reading of the corpus.)

> “October Eleventh” (AAL, 11 Oct 2015), a poem for National Coming Out Day

The italics were meant to indicat that this was actually a song rather a poem, similar to 2013's "Lyrics to the Song About" posts, and to the "music" tag posts with sheet music on the other blog. (Is "lyrics in italics" a real convention, or just something that seemed reasonable to me?) The sheet music for the _Ultimately Untrue Thought_ posts was done by a then-friend based on my singing.
