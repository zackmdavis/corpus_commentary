### Unnatural Categories Are Optimized for Deception
- Path: `AAL_content/2021/unnatural-categories-are-optimized-for-deception.md` · 2021-01-08 · philosophy (honesty, philosophy of language) · 10096 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2021/Jan/unnatural-categories-are-optimized-for-deception/
- Summary: LW-first. Followup to "Where to Draw the Boundaries?". "The one" argues categories are like national borders (Israel/Palestine two-state topology; links SSC "Categories Were Made for Man"), so trade categorizational nonawkwardness for "units of morality." Davis: that reading of "A Human's Guide to Words" skips the math. Categories are probabilistic models (hypotheses); redrawing a boundary changes predictions; non-epistemic reasons to prefer worse models = deception (of others) or wireheading (of self). Factory example: 128-entry blueness–eggness–vanadium joint distribution factorizes via naive Bayes (57 parameters); labels link models across machines; national-border metaphor "immediately breaks down as soon as you try to do any calculations" ("Countries aren't Bayesian networks"). Expected squared error: even/odd vs. 1–4/5–8 (5 vs 1.25); compact blegg categories cut error 13.63→1.38 vs. gerrymandered blegg* →4.12. Value-ladenness is lawful: values choose the space and metric, not the boundary ("How you should define words _depends on_ what you want, but that's _not_ the same as defining words any way you want"; poison example). Utility-function objection: Allais-style coherence; the gerrymandered category optimizes squared-error-minus-supplier-revenue — a moonlighting engineer's fraud; gold/silver receipt: reserving the right to lie p% ≡ adopting a less-accurate category system. Redefining "funny" to include one's jokes = wireheading via equivocation; "Yes requires the possibility of no." Mimicry: Batesian mimicry as functionally deceptive; asymmetric original/mimic; nondeceptive mimicry when nonmatching features are irrelevant (Anatidoid robot ducks, squirt guns, duck test) but "_fragile_." Meat substitutes and margarine-pink laws as category fights; CDC masks; "2 + 2 = 5" to save 3↑↑↑3 lives. Closing parable of the "Great Teacher" who says "using language in a way _you_ dislike, is not lying. You're not standing in defense of Truth if you insist on a word, brought explicitly into question, being used with some particular meaning" — "And you look up at the sky and see blue." Thanks Jessica Taylor, Abram Demski, Tsvi Benson-Tilsen.
- Themes: categorization, naive-bayes, information-theory, deception, wireheading, mimicry, value-laden-categories, gerrymandering, yudkowsky (Great Teacher allusion), scott-alexander (borders metaphor), philosophy-of-language
- Xrefs: UUT 2018 "Categories…"; AAL 2019 "Where to Draw…", "Algorithms of Deception!"; AAL 2020 "Message Length"; UUT 2020 "Memento Mori" (announced as the "mimicry" post); UUT 2023 "A Hill of Validity…" (the Great Teacher's words appear to adapt a specific public statement by Yudkowsky — verify there). LW thread (32 comments).
- Quotes:
> ... okay, I think I see the problem. I see how one might have gotten that out of "A Human's Guide to Words"—_if you skipped all the parts with math_.
> Because category "boundaries" are merely a visualization for a probabilistic model that makes predictions about the real world, you _can't_ "redraw the boundaries" associated with a communication signal without messing with the model that generates them, which means messing with your predictions about the real world.
> Agents that communicate in ways that systematically depart from efficient encodings are [better modeled as](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/sXHQ9R5tahiaXEZhR/algorithmic-intent-a-hansonian-generalized-anti-zombie) trying to deceive each other or wirehead themselves.
> Because the "national borders" metaphor is [just a metaphor](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/C4EjbrvG3PvZzizZb/failure-by-analogy). It _immediately_ breaks down as soon as you try to do any calculations.
> Countries aren't Bayesian networks. They just aren't.
> In this sense, the gerrymandered blegg\* concept is _quantitatively less informative_ than the original, compact blegg concept.
> But _given_ a metric on the variables that you care about predicting and using to inform predictions, which categories are cognitively useful depends on the the distribution of data in the world. You can't define a word any way you want.
> How you should define words _depends on_ what you want, but that's _not_ the same as defining words any way you want.
> [we just pay attention to the evidential entanglements](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/34XxbRFe54FycoCDw/the-bottom-line) instead of being [confused by words](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/KbjWLGJogCZY4HNsr/words-and-implications), then [there's no functional difference between](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YptSN8riyXJjJ8Qp8/maybe-lying-can-t-exist) saying "I reserve the right to lie _p_% of the time about whether something belongs to category _C_", and adopting a new, less-accurate category system that misclassifies _p_% of instances with respect to the old system.
> Depending on your goals, deception can be rational! If you _don't care_ about other agents having accurate models
> But precisely _because_ words don't have intrinsic meanings, there's no reason to _want_ to redefine an _existing_ word, _except_ to piggyback off the meaning people are _already_ using that signal for.
> A _mimic_ is an entity optimized to approximately match the distribution of the original in many, but not all traits, thereby being part of the same cluster as the original in some _subspace_ of the space the original category is defined in, but not the space as a whole.
> Nondeceptive mimicry is _fragile_, however: it works in contexts where the all the relevant features are ones where the mimic matches the original.
> What I _can_ say is that the hidden Bayesian structure of language and cognition makes no reference to the stakes, and departing from the structure [extracts a price](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wyyfFfaRar2jEdeQK/entangled-truths-contagious-lies) that [isn't up to us](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eY45uCCX7DdwJ4Jha/no-one-can-exempt-you-from-rationality-s-laws).
> And the Great Teacher says, "Some people I usually respect for their willingness to publicly die on a hill of facts, now seem to be talking as if color references are necessarily a factual statement about frequencies of light. But using language in a way _you_ dislike, is not lying. You're not standing in defense of Truth if you insist on a word, brought explicitly into question, being used with some particular meaning." And you look up at the sky and see blue.

### Communication Requires Common Interests or Differential Signal Costs

Perception needs technology only at the receiver; communication needs aligned sender/medium too; conventions arise only with common interests; deception parasitic on meaning, threatens to snowball; costly signaling (stotting gazelle) enables adversarial communication; Lachmann–Számadó–Bergstrom: departure need only be costly (social sanction). - Themes: signaling-theory, common-interest, costly-signals, deception, virtue-signaling

### Blood Is Thicker Than Water 🐬
- Path: `AAL_content/2021/blood-is-thicker-than-water.md` · 2021-09-27 · philosophy (philosophy of language) · 3905 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2021/Sep/blood-is-thicker-than-water/
- Summary: LW-first. "The one" (voicing Soares-like positions, incl. near-quotes) asks why care about phylogeny. Evolution actually happened; continuity postulate; genome at the root of the causal DAG; conditional independence (d-separation) is the deeper explanation of why categorization works ("_What_ space?"); learning new things (bone counts) shows genetic relatedness as a generator of similarities. "Finned swimmy animals" falls apart in more dimensions; "Blood is thicker than water." Skin in the game: veterinarian, sprain vs. stress fracture ("hurtankle"). Cats vs. dogs pen pal. Scott Alexander's report that the APA categorizes by symptoms, not cause — "In a sane world, this would be _utterly discrediting_ to the APA ... _we don't have a science of psychiatry_." Paraphyletic categories legitimate (connected sets in phylogenetic space); Aristotle already separated cetaceans from fish; *Fledermaus*. "The power to define concepts is the power to delimit thought." Thanks Tailcalled, John Wentworth.
- Themes: dolphins, phylogeny, causal-graphs, conditional-independence, natural-abstraction, psychiatry-critique, paraphyly, history-of-classification
- Quotes:
> Focusing on evolutionary relatedness is not an arbitrary æsthetic whim because evolution _actually happened_.
> "There's conditional independence structure in the causal graph" is a meaningfully _deeper_ explanation than "There's a cluster in configuration space", because conditional independence is what what makes it possible to _construct_ a "space" _such that_ there are clusters.
> Blood is thicker than water (that is, the similarities induced by shared blood are a thicker subspace of configuration space than the similarities induced by living in the water).
> In a sane world, this would be _utterly discrediting_ to the APA.
> But the claim that the distinction between fish and cetaceans (dolphins and whales) was only recognized after their differing evolutionary histories were discovered is just _false to historical fact_.
> The power to define concepts is the power to delimit thought, to determine what kinds of inferences are easily representable.

### Comment on "Deception as Cooperation"

- Summary: LW-first.
