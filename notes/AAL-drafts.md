### Accidentally Misleading People Should Be Embarrassing; Or, Higher Innocence
- Path: `AAL_content/drafts/accidentally-misleading-people-should-be-embarrassing-or-higher-innocence.md` · header date 2026-07-28 (links a 2026-08 post, so it was still in progress after that) · philosophy (honesty) · 1961 w · unpublished draft, public on Gitweb · nearly complete
- Summary: When true-ish statements lead reasonable people to false conclusions, the speaker should feel *embarrassment*, not defensiveness about "the innocence of one's soul". Listeners care whether they'll be misled again, not about moral blame. Embarrassment works as appeasement and social repair. Analogy to *marit ayin* (Jewish law forbids permissible acts that look like forbidden ones, e.g. hanging wet clothes on the sabbath), which denies norm-violators plausible deniability. Links "So You Don't Trust Me?" on costly signals. Anecdote: joining a work call unmuted while a housemate played a video game. Case study 1: Davis suspected an "ideological rival" of misrepresenting an essay, because a Ctrl-F search came up empty. A third party showed that the search depended on browser focus inside the embedded PDF viewer. "I was _so embarrassed_." Seven years later he notes the essay was six pages long. Case study 2 (left anonymous in the text, but the link goes to the 24 June 2025 section of "Conversations With Cade Metz on the Rationalists", so the journalist is Cade Metz and the source is Kelsey Piper): a paraphrase that the source "somehow prove statistically which side was right"; the journalist "didn't seem embarrassed". Davis grants that the journalist may have been "innocent in his soul" but argues that "the moral law is unmercifully permissive about everyone else's right to _avoid unlucky people_." Coda, "Higher Innocence": a false ending, then a turn credited to prereader Said Achmiz. Was it really an accident? Quoting the Twelve Virtues on "permit me to believe" vs. "force me to believe": motivated search means misleading in the direction of one's bottom line isn't wholly accidental. Turned on himself: "Six pages? Really?"
- Themes: honesty, misleading-vs-lying, accountability, motivated-reasoning, self-correction, journalism, cade-metz, social-emotions, said-achmiz
- Xrefs: AAL 2026 "So You Don't Trust Me?"; AAL 2025 "The Relationship Between Social Punishment and Shared Maps"; AAL 2019 "Firming Up Not-Lying Around Its Edge-Cases…"; LW 2026 "Conversations With Cade Metz on the Rationalists" (and the 2024/2025 Metz interview posts); AAL 2026 "Contra Pace on When to Apologize" (regret cluster).
- Quotes:
> Mostly, people don't care about judging the innocence of your soul; they care about whether they'll be led into falsehood the next time they take the risk of believing what you say—if there is a next time.
> I was _so embarrassed_.
> (Recounting this seven years later, I went back and downloaded the PDF. Excluding the title page and the endnotes, it was six pages.)
> Unlucky accidents do not impinge upon the innocence of one's soul under the moral law, but the moral law is unmercifully permissive about everyone else's right to _avoid unlucky people_.
> (Not because I didn't try, but because my prereader Said Achmiz didn't let me get away with it.)
> If you have a [bottom line](https://www.readthesequences.com/The-Bottom-Line) that you want to believe, and you go around looking for evidence that permits you to believe it, I really have to question in what sense it's an "accident" if you end up misleading people towards your bottom line.
> I wasn't lying, and that's a _kind_ of innocence, but the cold light of "Six pages? Really?" makes me want to aspire to a higher form of innocence.

### Repentance Is Not Compensation
- Path: `AAL_content/drafts/repentance-is-not-compensation.md` · header date placeholder (follows the June 2026 Pace post) · philosophy · Tags: regret · 678 w · unpublished draft, public · opening + bracketed TODO outline
- Summary: Opens by block-quoting his own "Contra Pace on When to Apologize" (the smallest ask for forgiveness is a policy change, not being "made whole"). The TODO outline: a skeptic invokes the vNM continuity axiom (some payment should always make B whole; airline overbooking as an ordinary commercial example). Davis proposes to "taboo 'apology'" and split it into *compensation* and *repentance*. Trusted relationships roll over small balances, and what an apology there restores is trust, through credible evidence that the wrongdoer has learned the lesson. An apology gift is a costly *signal* of intent to change, not a payment sized to the harm. The example: someone who injures a friend's voice and says "Sorry, but his voice was just so annoying" stays an enemy: "I'm not going to be bought off."
- Themes: apology, regret, decision-theory, vnm-rationality, trust, taboo-your-words
- Xrefs: AAL 2026 "Contra Pace on When to Apologize"; UUT 2020 "Liability"; AAL draft "Accidentally Misleading…".
- Quotes:
> We should taboo "apology" and think of two things that both might result in someone saying "I'm sorry": compensation, and repentence.
> There might be an apology gift, which superficially looks like compensation, but the difference is that the gift is a mere costly _signal_ of intent to change rather than being precisely sized to cancel out the harm.
> If it's like, "Sorry, but his voice was just so annoying. I still like you, though", then you're still an enemy; I'm not going to be bought off.

### Acausal Trade as Limiting Case
- Path: `AAL_content/drafts/acausal-trade-as-limiting-case.md` · header date placeholder · philosophy · Tags: decision theory · 320 w · unpublished draft, public · one-sentence opening + TODO outline
- Summary: Acausal trade isn't spooky. Every deal involves *inferring* the other party's terms; even a contract's words have to be interpreted ("the symbols themselves are inert"). Explicitness comes in degrees: contract → swap meet ("Trade?") → prisoners coordinating a riot with "a knowing look". Acausal trade is the limit with no physical signal at all, "_all_ inference".
- Themes: decision-theory, acausal-trade, continuity-arguments, demystification, coordination
- Xrefs: AAL decision-theory posts; the continuum style of argument also appears in the categorization posts.
- Quotes:
> (And no matter how explicit a contract is, it's still your brain that has to interpret the words and infer the meaning; the symbols themselves are inert.)
> Acausal trade is just the limiting case where there's _no_ physical communication, and it's _all_ inference.
