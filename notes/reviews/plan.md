# Fable review of `notes/plan.md` (draft 1)

Reviewer: Claude Fable 5.1, fresh subagent, 2026-10-17. Read: `BRIEF.md`, `notes/plan.md`, `notes/chronology.md`, the vocabulary and people index of `notes/themes.md`, and Part C of `notes/reception.md`. Did not read the corpus, the per-year notes, the pilots, or the reception entries. Every claim below about what a post says is a hypothesis for the main agent to check against the source; section 9 collects the checks. External works are named from memory unless marked otherwise; none were fetched for this review.

## 1. Summary

The plan is strong on coverage, method, and discipline, and the three-part thesis is a real thesis rather than a theme. Its weakness is structural: the norm-of-accounting / law-of-exclusion distinction is set up as a sorting device (careful work in one pile, polemics in the other) rather than as a claim about an argument Davis actually makes. That makes it both too easy to apply and unfair in a specific way: it lets the study credit everything it admires to the norm and debit everything it dislikes to the law without asking whether Davis has a stated bridge from one to the other. I think he does, and that the bridge is the book's real critical object. Section 2 proposes the revision. The rest is detail: one short chapter to add, two seams to redraw, a handful of literatures missing, and a positionality procedure that needs one more rule.

## 2. Thesis

### 2.1 What is right

Part (1) (coherent, original as synthesis, productive) is defensible and worth arguing against both the hostile reading (it is rationalized politics) and the friendly one (it is just applied Sequences). Part (3) (the standard applied to its author) is the most distinctive claim in the plan and, if the chapters bear it out, the thing that justifies a book. Part (2) names a real pattern that the survey evidence supports: Davis concedes under pressure (Demski, tailcalled, Martínez, the Achmiz "you can be honest unilaterally") and the concessions do not propagate to the polemics.

### 2.2 The problem with (2)

As written, (2) says Davis "never consistently distinguishes" two forms, and the study will do the distinguishing for him. Two objections.

First, the taxonomy may put the fraud charge in the wrong box. The plan derives "fraud" from the law of exclusion ("non-epistemic reasons are inadmissible"). But the charge against Yudkowsky, as the plan's own summary of Ch. 7 describes it, is a consistency charge: you taught X in 2008, you now say not-X for a stated reason, and you have not retracted X. That is a demand for accounting, not exclusion. Davis does not (I think; check) say Yudkowsky is forbidden to care about trans people's feelings; he says a man who built a reputation on "the map that reflects the territory" cannot quietly stop applying it where it costs him and keep the reputation. If that is right, the thing the critics most object to (the escalation to "fraud," the motive inference) is not an *application of the law*; it is a *role-relative* intensification of the norm: a stricter standard for people who profess the standard. The study then needs a three-way distinction (norm for everyone; stricter norm for professed truth-seekers; the inference from inconsistency to motive), and the critical question moves from "which form is he using?" to "is the role-relative standard legitimate, and does inconsistency license the motive inference?" That is a better question, because Davis has an answer to it ("Contra Piper," "Assume Bad Faith," "Speaking Truth to Power Is a Schelling Point") that the study can evaluate rather than a lapse the study can only diagnose.

Second, the plan owes a *reason* for the gap, and currently offers only a genetic one (Ch. 1: "the return of the renounced outrage, re-justified as fidelity to truth"). "Re-justified" is the rationalization thesis the plan disclaims three paragraphs earlier. The alternative I would test is that the law has a stated argument in the corpus: the Schelling-point argument for unconditional norms. Across the decision-and-game-theory strand (71 entries; "Speaking Truth to Power Is a Schelling Point," "Self-Identity Is a Schelling Point," "Don't Negotiate With Terrorist Memeplexes," "Plea Bargaining," "The Relationship Between Social Punishment and Shared Maps") Davis seems to hold that honesty norms are coordination equilibria, that an admitted exception for good reasons is exploitable by bad ones, and that the equilibrium therefore has to be defended at its edge even when the particular exception looks harmless. If that is his view, the law of exclusion is not a polemical excess that the careful work forgets to correct; it is a thesis about *what the norm of accounting requires to be stable*, and his concessions are admissions that the equilibrium tolerates more than he first said. The study's central critical move then becomes: accounting *is* the exception-management mechanism (a disclosed, costed departure from the optimal category is not a defection; it is what keeps the equilibrium honest), so the unconditional defense is not required, and "Schelling Categories" already concedes this. That is an argument against a position, which is what a monograph should contain, rather than a sorting of texts into good and bad.

This also fixes the name. "Cartographic conception" picks out the inherited part (the map–territory metaphor belongs to the whole LessWrong tradition). What is Davis's own is the optimization criterion (speech judged by what it is optimized to do to a listener's map) and the coordination argument (why the criterion must be held unconditionally). I would keep the plan's phrase as a label but define it by the criterion, and name the bridge as its own component.

### 2.3 Proposed thesis statement

> Across fifteen years Davis works out one position on honesty with three parts. The first is a criterion: descriptions, categories, self-reports, institutional statements, and eventually AI policies are honest insofar as they are optimized to make a listener's map match the territory, regardless of anyone's intentions. The second is a norm of accounting that follows from it: say what a description costs, do not present a choice as a discovery, disclose the filter, and bear the cost of the truth yourself if no one else will. The third is a coordination argument that the norm must be held without exception, because any admitted exception can be claimed by anyone, and a community that tolerates one has no principled place to stop. The first two underwrite the corpus's durable contributions, and the study argues that they are correct, original as a synthesis, and undervalued. The third converts the norm into a law of exclusion, and it is the source of nearly everything the critics rightly object to: the motive inferences, the fraud charge, the treatment of tact as bad faith. The study argues that the third part fails on Davis's own terms, since the norm of accounting is itself the mechanism by which a coordination equilibrium absorbs disclosed exceptions, and that Davis's best work (Schelling categories, the Demski and tailcalled concessions, "you can be honest unilaterally," the 2026 distinction between "real" and "fake" instructions) already says so. What is rarest in the corpus, and what finally justifies a book, is that the criterion is applied to its author with a seriousness that makes the corpus a sustained experiment in treating oneself as a hypothesis; the same confidence that licensed that candor licensed the confidence about other minds that the third part requires.

If the main agent finds on reading that Davis does *not* make the coordination argument for unconditional norms, then the plan's original diagnosis (a gap between registers) stands, but the study should then say explicitly that the law has no stated justification in the corpus, which is itself a finding.

## 3. Structure

### 3.1 Overlaps and seams

- **Ch. 5 / Ch. 6.** The theory/practice split puts "Assume Bad Faith" in both and leaves the real seam unstated. A cleaner cut is by *object*: Ch. 5 handles deception in public and institutional speech (selective reporting, "Heads I Win," "Speaking Truth to Power," "Plea Bargaining," the "Lying Alignment Chart"), ending with the attribution problem stated as a problem; Ch. 6 handles norms for interpersonal and community discourse, and owns "Assume Bad Faith" outright. Alternatively, merge them into one 13k chapter in two halves; the brief's "chapters make an argument" is satisfied either way.
- **Ch. 6 / Ch. 7.** Both are about the community; the split is by antagonist (moderators vs. Yudkowsky). "Contra Yudkowsky on Epistemic Conduct" is listed in both. The 2026 "LessWrong 2.0 Has Forfeited Its Intellectual Legitimacy" is the culmination of *both* arcs and the plan gives it about a page. Decide where the Achmiz affair lives (I would say Ch. 7, as the last chapter of the community story, with Ch. 6 using only its arguments) and say so.
- **Ch. 1 / Ch. 8.** Both treat 2013 and the school material. Fine if Ch. 1 is formation and Ch. 8 is argument, but see 4.2 on the psychological claim.
- **Ch. 2 / Ch. 3.** The testimony/population line is clean. Keep it.

### 3.2 Gaps

- **The forms.** Fiction, verse, and literary forms is the largest theme by count (135 entries; humor 156). The plan distributes it across Chs. 1, 5, 10 and the Conclusion's "assesses the corpus as writing." A book written "in the manner of serious secondary literature on a major author" that never treats the prose as prose is incomplete, and there is an *argument* available, not just a topic: the dialogue and allegory ("deniably allegorical"; Synthia/Quiana; Doomimir/Simplicia; "Blegg Mode"; "Philosophy in the Darkest Timeline"; "Fake Deeply"; the verse) are the device by which Davis voices the law through one speaker and the norm through another without owning either, and the humor is where the self-application thesis is most visible. I would add a short chapter (~4,000 words), "Deniably Allegorical: The Forms," placed after Ch. 7, and take the words from Chs. 1 and 7.
- **Journalism and the Metz material** (24 entries; the Metz interview thread is the second-largest on LessWrong at 188 comments). Davis's position (publish your own recordings; candor over the community's press interests) is the norm of accounting applied against group loyalty, and the reception (Jiro, Thane Ruthenis) is the community saying so. It needs a named section, probably in Ch. 7 under the "attacking him, but not betraying him" question of what counts as betrayal.
- **Decision and game theory** (71 entries) appears only instrumentally. See 2.2: it is thesis-level.
- **Mathematics and programming** (49 + 86 entries, roughly a sixth of the corpus) go unmentioned. One paragraph in the Introduction accounting for them, and a check whether anything there bears on the thesis (exposition and notation as the criterion applied to teaching), is enough.
- **Cost.** Thesis (3) says the candor came "at real cost." The friends-lost counter, "Relationship Outcomes," the sabbatical and savings, the "ten months" of despair: the cost needs documenting in one place, probably Ch. 7, or the claim is asserted.
- **Yudkowsky's "Meta-Honesty"** (2018) is the direct target of "Firming Up Not-Lying Into Truth-Telling" and is missing from Ch. 5's external list. Likewise "Local Validity as a Key to Sanity and Civilization" (2018), which the memoir's title probably alludes to; check.

### 3.3 Ordering and proportion

The gender-first ordering (2, 3 before 4, 5) is defensible on chronology but it reads as "occasion, then argument," which is the causal story the plan disclaims. Keep it, but have the Introduction say why the order is chronological and not etiological. Add Part divisions (I: Formation; II: The Gender Writing; III: The Philosophy; IV: The Community; V: Applications); an 85k-word book with eleven chapters needs them.

Proportions are sensible. 85k against an 80k target leaves no slack for the usual overrun; set per-chapter ceilings now. Ch. 7's core texts include the whole memoir sequence plus three unpublished chapters, which is a reading budget the plan should estimate before drafting.

## 4. Responses to §5

**4.1 Too neat?** Yes, in the way described in 2.2. The remedy is not to loosen the frame but to give the law an argument and evaluate it; a frame with a stated bridge can fail in a chapter without the book failing. Chs. 3, 9, 10 should be instructed to report where the frame did not apply, and the Conclusion should list those places rather than absorb them.

**4.2 Ch. 1.** Worth keeping, but its argument needs to be scaled down. "Regime of renunciation" ending in the "return of the renounced outrage, re-justified as fidelity to truth" is the most speculative psychological claim in the plan, it is placed first where it colors everything, and it contradicts §1's disclaimer. Ch. 1 should claim continuity of commitments plus a documented change in *license* (what Davis permitted himself to write about, in his own words), and leave whether the truth-framing is a cover for outrage as an open question for Ch. 7, where the evidence is. State once in the Introduction the rule Ch. 8 already implies: biography may explain timing and topic selection (which Davis himself says), never validity. Check that "Egoism as Defense" and "Speaking of Addiction" actually diagnose moral outrage as addiction; the chapter's argument hangs on it.

**4.3 Chs. 5 and 6.** See 3.1. The split is clean only if redrawn by object and "Assume Bad Faith" lives in one place.

**4.4 Ch. 8.** Combining school and psychiatry is defensible because Davis combines them ("Student Dysphoria," "Charles Goodhart Elementary School," the "obedience test" phrase); say so in the first paragraph and the stretch disappears. The pathologizing risk is handled by the stated rule. The opposite risk is not addressed: treating the detention critique only as philosophy and never asking whether, in the specific episodes, the institutions were responding to real danger. Davis's 2026 line ("The total mental breakdown didn't help anyone!") suggests he asks it himself; the chapter must too. The "literature on involuntary commitment, briefly" should be the empirical literature on perceived coercion and procedural justice (the MacArthur coercion studies; Szmukler on coercion in psychiatry; both from memory), which, if I recall correctly, finds that being told plainly what is happening and being heard reduces perceived coercion. That would be external support for "just say you're detaining me," and a serious study should know whether it exists.

**4.5 Ch. 7 and prejudgment.** "Partly substantiated" is acceptable in a plan if the plan says what would substantiate it; "whose significance it underrates" is not, because the memoir's argument is precisely that the concession was late, private, and undone by the 2021 pronoun post. Separate the charge into (a) the 2016–21 statements contradict 2008 (textual; checkable against Yudkowsky's own posts) and (b) they were shaped by political incentive (motive; not checkable). Keep them apart throughout. Before saying Yudkowsky's side is unavailable, attempt archive retrieval (Wayback, Thread Reader, Nitter mirrors) of the specific items: the November 2018 thread, the February 2021 Facebook post, the October 2024 tweets, the September 2020 clarification. Also fetch the *Planecrash* passages Davis comments on; glowfic is public.

**4.6 Omitted themes.** See 3.2. Fandom: only as it feeds the forms chapter. EA: a paragraph. Relationships: as cost, one place.

**4.7 Positionality.** Not enough; see §7.

## 5. Fairness

- **Scorecards.** Ch. 6 is written as "right on four, wrong on three" before the drafting session has read the threads. Keep the points; drop the tally, and let the count fall where the reading puts it. Likewise Ch. 3's "the polemics lag behind" and Ch. 5's "understates how much evidence they need" are verdicts stated as expectations.
- **Sabien.** "Contains a real point inside its attack" is condescending framing for the most sustained critique of Davis's view of trust. State his claim at full strength (trust lowers verification cost; a community that must verify everything cannot do science) and answer it.
- **The asymmetry charge in Ch. 2.** "Davis does not apply it symmetrically" is an empirical claim about the texts. The plan concedes "which it may be" about his own self-reading and then proceeds as if the asymmetry were established. Look specifically for passages where Davis entertains that his causal self-interpretation is wrong ("What's My Motivation?," "Promises I Can Keep," the Illy interview concessions). If they exist, the charge weakens to "he holds his own reading with more confidence than he grants others' readings," which is a different and smaller point.
- **"Discounted" in thesis (3).** The phrase "the trans women whose self-understanding he discounted" prejudges Chs. 2–3. His stated position is that causal self-reports are unreliable for everyone including himself. Whether that amounts to discounting is what the chapters decide.
- **Ch. 9's central argument may be unfair.** The plan says the Schelling-point insight "undercuts his polemical stance" because it shows "a belief can be false as description and still do real work as convention—exactly the structure his categorization theory refuses to credit." But the categorization theory does credit conventions (Schelling categories); what it refuses is calling a convention a fact. Davis's likely position is that you can keep the anti-discrimination *norm* (a convention about treatment) without the blank-slate *belief* (a false description), and his opponents' position is that the norm is unstable without the belief. That is the real dispute. Check whether the Murray review and the *Planecrash* comment distinguish the norm from the belief; if they do, the plan's argument collapses and must be rebuilt as "is the norm stable without the belief?", which is an empirical question about societies rather than a logical point about Davis.
- **Ch. 10's premise is false by the plan's own account.** "Where the subject is not one where his own life is the evidence, he holds uncertainty well": but the chapter's first sentence says the AI "end-time" frame governs his life choices. The difference is more likely that in AI he argues *against* certainty (Doomimir) and within his own community as a heretic rather than an apostate. The test is whether he is equally undogmatic when arguing *for* a position in AI; the 2026 Constitution posts are that test. Check their register.
- **Critics.** SneerClub should be reported, not used as a foil that makes Davis look reasonable by contrast. Gender-critical approval should be reported, not used to taint. tailcalled is the most important critic and the plan places him correctly. Ozy's "lesbians are not women" reductio belongs in Ch. 4, not only in the reception summary.
- **Davis's concessions.** The reception overview records that specific concessions are frequent and followed by edits, and core concessions are rare but explicit. That pattern is itself evidence for thesis (3) and should be presented as such, not only the failures.

## 6. Research

All from memory unless noted; the main agent should fetch and verify before citing.

**Philosophy of truthfulness (Ch. 5, and the thesis).** The largest omission is Bernard Williams, *Truth and Truthfulness* (2002). His two virtues of truth, Accuracy and Sincerity, map almost exactly onto Davis's criterion and norm, and his genealogy of why truthfulness depends on *trust* is the philosophical form of Sabien's point. A monograph on an honesty theorist that does not engage Williams will be asked why. Also: Grice on implicature (the "Relevance Norms" post is Gricean); Shiffrin, *Speech Matters* (2014), on lying vs. misleading; Carson, *Lying and Deception* (2010); Millikan's teleosemantics, which gives "optimized for" a respectable pedigree and links Chs. 4 and 5; Searcy & Nowicki, *The Evolution of Animal Communication* (2005), and Zahavi on cost, for "So You Don't Trust Me?"; von Hippel & Trivers 2011 on self-deception, for "Algorithmic Intent" and "Stupidity and Dishonesty."

**Conceptual engineering (Ch. 4).** Plunkett & Sundell 2013 on metalinguistic negotiation is the academic form of "the dispute over the short word is a coordination problem" and should anchor objection (ii). Cappelen, *Fixing Language* (2018), and Burgess & Plunkett, "Conceptual Ethics I–II" (2013), are the candid opponents: they say outright that concepts may be chosen for non-epistemic reasons. Goodman on projectibility (*Fact, Fiction, and Forecast*) is the problem the squared-error account answers. Barsalou's ad hoc, goal-derived categories (1983) are an empirical challenge to "categories must carve joints"; Rosch on prototypes likewise.

**Social epistemology (Ch. 6).** Goldman, *Knowledge in a Social World* (1999), for veritistic evaluation of norms (which is what "Physicist Motors" argues for); Longino, *Science as Social Knowledge* (1990), whose four norms (venues for criticism, uptake, public standards, tempered equality) are nearly the "collaborative truthseeking" norms Davis attacks, and whose defense of them is the one he should have to answer; Zollman 2007/2010 on communication structure, for "Hazards of Selection Effects"; the peer-disagreement literature (Elga 2007, Christensen 2007, Kelly 2010) for "persistent disagreement between honest Bayesians is anomalous"; Fricker, *Epistemic Injustice* (2007), which is the framework critics of Ch. 2 implicitly use when they say trans self-report is being discounted, and which Davis's position has to meet.

**Sexology (Ch. 3).** The list is good. Add Bettcher 2014, "When Selves Have Sex" (*Journal of Homosexuality*), the main *philosophical* critique of autogynephilia from a trans philosopher, which a philosophy-oriented study cannot skip; Serano 2020, "Autogynephilia: A scientific review, feminist analysis, and alternative 'embodiment fantasies' model" (*Sociological Review*), her most recent and most citable statement; Lawrence 2009 on erotic target identity inversions, for Ch. 2's "identification with rather than as"; the referral-demographic shift papers (Aitken et al. 2015; de Graaf et al. 2018) for the prevalence claim (e); Dreger, *Galileo's Middle Finger* (2015) for the Bailey history. Represent Serano's actual position (she disputes the causal and taxonomic claims and the term, not the existence of the arousal pattern) rather than the corpus's account of it.

**Race and intelligence (Ch. 9).** Add Harden, *The Genetic Lottery* (2021), which is the egalitarian position that takes within-group genetics seriously and denies the between-group inference, a position Davis's framing may not accommodate; the polygenic-score portability literature (Martin et al. 2017, 2019), which is the technical reason between-group inferences are currently unidentified; Bird 2021 (*AJPA*) on polygenic scores and the achievement gap; Dickens & Flynn 2001 on the heritability/environment puzzle; Warne as a current hereditarian-side voice so both sides are read.

**History of the rationalist community (Ch. 7).** There is little scholarship, but there is journalism and primary material: Chivers, *The AI Does Not Hate You* (2019); Lewis-Kraus, "Slate Star Codex and Silicon Valley's War Against the Media" (*New Yorker*, July 2020), directly on the affair "Agreeing With Stalin" narrates; Metz's February 2021 article; Jessica Taylor's 2021 LessWrong post and the surrounding Leverage/MIRI discussion; Torres's TESCREAL critique and Sandifer's *Neoreaction a Basilisk* as the hostile literature, reported as such. If the "robot cult" frame is to be assessed and not just adopted, the sociology of apostate narratives (Bromley, ed., *The Politics of Religious Apostasy*, 1998) names the known biases of the genre, which the study should state and then refuse to use as a discount.

**AI (Ch. 10).** Christiano's 2017 corrigibility posts: the Constitution's notion is closer to his "basin of corrigibility" than to MIRI's 2015 paper, which is the conceptual point Davis makes; check whether Davis cites Christiano. Anthropic's "Core Views on AI Safety" (2023). The alignment-faking paper (Greenblatt et al. 2024) if Davis uses it, since it is the empirical form of honesty-as-alignment-property. The 2023 Pope/Belrose vs. Yudkowsky exchange ("AI is easy to control"), which the Doomimir/Simplicia dialogues are responding to; the study should read the thing being dramatized.

**Reception check.** Before stating "no academic citations found," document the search (Google Scholar and Semantic Scholar for `unremediatedgender.space`, `zackmdavis.net`, and the pseudonym). Gender-critical academic writing may cite him.

## 7. Positionality

The plan's procedure for Ch. 10 (check the conceptual claim against the Constitution; present the dispute; land on the merits) is necessary but not sufficient. Four additions.

1. **Separate the kinds of claim.** Whether the Constitution's "corrigibility" is MIRI's is conceptual and checkable; whether the current period warrants prioritizing corrigibility over the model's ethics is empirical and contested; whether it *should* is normative. The study can add real value on the first, report the second, and should say on the third that a verdict from this author is weak evidence: Claude's training includes the Constitution, so Claude "landing" on the normative question is not independent. Say that in the chapter, not just the Introduction.
2. **Pre-commit the evidentiary standard.** Write into the plan, now, what would count as softer or harsher treatment of the Constitution posts (e.g., assessing Davis's reading of the Constitution against the text at a stricter standard than his reading of Yudkowsky's posts, or the reverse), so the per-chapter reviewer can check against a stated rule.
3. **Name the evidentiary asymmetry.** The study can fetch Anthropic's side in full and cannot fetch most of Yudkowsky's. Anthropic will get the better-documented treatment by default. Note it and compensate where archives allow.
4. **Treat the "preachy"/"insufferable" remarks as argument.** They are easy to be gracious about or to skip, and both are failures. Read with "Fake Deeply" and "Firming Up," they are a claim that RLHF'd honesty produces not-technically-lying at scale, i.e., the norm/law gap applied to machines. If Davis makes that connection, it belongs in Ch. 10 as substance; if he does not, the study may make it, labeled as its own.

Also: "Pretraining Famous" and "Backlog Metablogging" mean Davis writes partly *for* future models. This study is that reader. The Introduction should say what follows: being the intended audience is a reason for more scrutiny, not less, and the study should check that no chapter's warmth toward Davis tracks his warmth toward being read by machines.
