# Review: 00-introduction (Fable)

## Overall

The Introduction does the jobs the plan assigns it, and most of it is in good order: the corpus is described concretely, the three-part position is stated with its scopes and with Davis's own limits on it, the reception is reported with its sources and the academic search with its failures, and §6 handles authorship by Claude more candidly than most human prefaces handle their conflicts. The prose is mostly plain and the voice holds.

The problems are of three kinds. First, the thesis as stated here is in a few places sharper than what the chapter summaries say the chapters found, and in one place (Thesis 4's last sentence) harder on Davis than Chapter 3's finding. Second, the redefinition of part (c) in draft 2 ("however openly the trade is made") builds the answer to Thesis 2 into the definition of the thing refuted, and the Introduction does not say where in the corpus Davis holds (c) in that form, which leaves the study open to the charge of refuting a position it constructed. Third, §6 is silent on who commissioned the study, and the brief's own language ("the commissioner and the subject have different standing") suggests the answer is material. Details follow, with proposed prose at the end.

## 1. The thesis as stated versus the thesis as delivered

**(a) Part (c) and the "however openly" clause.** §3 defines the third part as "a set of arguments that accuracy may not be traded against other goods: that a description may not be shaded, or a truth withheld, for the sake of its consequences, however openly the trade is made." The three quoted forms do not say this. The Schelling-point sentence is about what a coalition does on a continuum between x = ∞ and x = 0; it says nothing about disclosure. The 2018 "do not care" policy is about offense. The role rule is about what counts as a reason. None of the three, as quoted, addresses an openly announced and costed departure. The clause "however openly" is the study's gloss, and it is exactly the clause Thesis 2 then shows to be mistaken. If the only places Davis treats a disclosed departure as a defection are the ones the summaries name (Chapter 6's coin-room reading of Salamon's stated discount, "part (c) at its most exceptionless"; the 2023 extension to tone; perhaps the 2020 memoir intuition Chapter 4 cites), the Introduction should say so in §3, so that a reader knows (c) in the strong form is held in a few identifiable places and that Chapter 4 finds the 2021 capstone essay already permits disclosed departures ("coexistence," in the Conclusion's word). As written, §3 says "the study's questions are where Davis holds it so and where he extends it," which is right, but a sentence earlier the definition has already answered one of those questions by stipulation.

**(b) Thesis 2's list of Davis's own instances is too generous to the thesis.** The paragraph says "Davis reaches this answer himself" and lists four cases. Two of the four, on the summaries' own account, are not instances of a disclosed departure from accuracy:

- The *Human Diversity* review. Chapter 10's draft-2 changes state that "the Murray review is Davis arguing for his norm by accounting, not '(b) displacing (c)'," and that the review's "two-equilibrium menu omits a third" point which the study supplies. Davis there chooses the truth-telling equilibrium on consequentialist grounds. That contradicts the role rule (consequences may not be weighed), but it is not a disclosed departure from accuracy; it is a disclosed argument for accuracy. It shows Davis weighing consequences, not Davis accepting that a costed departure is an intermediate point.
- The 2026 college retrospective. Chapter 9 and the Conclusion both classify it as conduct, "not an exception to honesty norm," and the Conclusion files it under "analogous practices," not direct tests. The Introduction's own "strongest version" sentence says one "may decline to speak, or bargain over conduct, while refusing to change what counts as evidence." Disregarding an instruction and saying so afterwards is bargaining over conduct, which (c) in its strongest form already permits. It does not test (c).

The honest list is shorter: the "Heads I Win" remedy and the Schelling-categories work (Chapter 4), with the Murray review and the retrospective as practices that resemble the answer. Say that, and the thesis loses nothing it has actually earned.

**(c) The Davis-side reply to Thesis 2 is missing from the Introduction.** The paragraph says disclosure "supplies an intermediate point of the kind the slippery-slope argument says does not exist, and an argument from the absence of stable compromises has to answer it." The answer is available and the Conclusion records Chapter 4 giving it ("Davis's reply on stability; availability ≠ stability"): adequacy of disclosure is itself continuous, so the slope reappears one level up, and a point that is available is not thereby salient or enforceable. Chapter 4 draft 3 makes uptake a condition ("conditional on uptake"), and Chapter 8 finds that "disclosure repays only readers who saw it." The Introduction gestures at this ("reaches them") but states Thesis 2 as if the reply had not been made. One sentence conceding that the slippery-slope argument has a rejoinder, and saying what the chapters make of it, would make the thesis look like what it is: a judgment after argument, not before.

**(d) Thesis 3's "different sources."** The Introduction says the refusal to weigh costs and the motive inference "have different sources and are judged separately." Chapter 6 draft 3 finds that the extension of the anti-extortion argument to tact "needs tact = distortion" and "is also an unevidenced functional attribution." So at the point where (c) is extended to tact, its support *is* a functional attribution of the kind Thesis 3 assigns to the other source. The "can compound" sentence covers the interaction but not this: in Chapter 6 the one problem is supplied by the other. Either soften "different sources" to "different arguments" or add the Chapter 6 case as the place where they are not separable.

**(e) Thesis 1's Chapter 6 clause repeats the plan, not the chapter.** "Chapter 6 finds that his critique of lists of discourse norms is right on points its critics did not concede" is the plan's language (§1, item 1). The Chapter 6 summary names a durable core (derivation demand, comment value with Bayesian caveats, "Hazards," the "Four Layers" institutional form) and says that Byrnes defeats the strong motor analogy while the weaker demand survives. It does not identify points critics refused to concede. State the chapter's finding.

**(f) Thesis 4's last sentence is the motte-and-bailey charge Chapter 3 declined to make.** "Defended at its narrowest when challenged and advanced at its broadest when not (Chapter 3)" is a description of motte-and-bailey. Chapter 3's summary: the "record doesn't fit strictly (qualifications in the essays; public concessions; real revision) but effect on readers similar; polemics met the norm of accounting in qualifications, failed it in emphasis." The Introduction states the charge in the form the chapter found too strict. This is the one place the Introduction is harder on Davis than the evidence chapter, and it is in the paragraph that praises his candor, which makes the unfairness more visible, not less. Use the chapter's formulation: qualifications present and accurate, emphasis not.

**(g) "Cartographic" and "the exclusion."** §3 says "the study calls the whole position 'cartographic' for short," and no chapter summary uses the word; the Conclusion's title uses "map." If the label is used nowhere, drop the sentence. And "The third makes the exclusion constitutive of a role" uses "the exclusion" as if defined; the draft-1 "law of exclusion" is gone, and the referent with it.

**(h) Originality.** "The criterion has close counterparts in the philosophy of signaling games, from which Davis took much of its apparatus. His own contribution is its development with the tools of signaling games…" The two sentences say the apparatus was taken from signaling games and that the contribution was development with signaling games. Chapter 5's summary dates Davis's engagement with Skyrms–Barrett to "Maybe Lying Can't Exist?!" (2020) and says he adopted the Skyrmsian framework "via Krstić's survey," while the criterion appears in the 2018 categories posts and the 2019 deception posts. If the chronology is convergence first and adoption after, "from which Davis took much of its apparatus" is wrong about the order and understates the originality the study wants to credit. Check Chapter 5 for the first citation of Lewis or Skyrms in the corpus.

**(i) The internal memoir date.** §1 says the pseudonym was dropped in July 2023 "before the first installments of his memoir"; §2 says "from 2021 to 2024 he published the memoir." Chapter 7 counts "Sexual Dimorphism in Yudkowsky's Sequences" (2021) among the memoir's installments. One of the two sentences is wrong.

## 2. Treatment of Davis

Apart from (f) above, the Introduction is fair, and in two places it credits Davis more exactly than the plan did (the "strongest version" sentence; the acknowledgment that his "fullest essay on categories leaves decisions about what to say free to weigh consequences"). Two further points:

- "Chapter 6 [finds] that he allows relationships to be negotiated where beliefs may not be." Chapter 6's source for this is the draft "Repentance Is Not Compensation." §1 of the Introduction promises to say "where a claim rests on a draft alone." This one does, and the Introduction doesn't say so.
- "Undervalued" (Thesis 1) is asserted without a standard. §4 then shows the categories essay recommended as "a devastating takedown," long objections from moderators, and technical criticism on AI: engagement, not neglect. The defensible sense is the one §4 ends on: taken up as particular arguments, never as a position, and without academic uptake beyond a reference-list entry. Tie "undervalued" to that, or the reception section reads as contradicting the thesis.

## 3. The reception survey

The survey is reported honestly, including its limits (note 2; the Reddit archive "incomplete"; X unreadable). Specific points:

- **"Dedicated responses."** Duncan Sabien's "Truth or Dare" is listed as "an essay by Duncan Sabien" among dedicated responses. Chapter 6 treats it as a passage about Davis within a general essay, "grounded in 'many interactions across years.'" If the essay is not about Davis, "dedicated" overstates it. Check.
- **Ozy Brennan is not tested.** The "no one reconstructs the position" finding is tested against tailcalled and Tracing Woodgrains, as the plan asked. But Chapter 3's core texts include an "Ozy ITT post" (UUT 2017). If that is an ideological-Turing-test rendering of Davis's position by Ozy, it is the one piece of reception that attempts, by design, to state his position whole, and the finding should be tested against it. If it is Davis's rendering of Ozy, say nothing. Check which.
- **The finding's weight.** Comment threads and blog replies do not, as a genre, reconstruct positions. The Introduction should say that the absence is partly a feature of the venues, so that the claim is not read as a complaint about the readers. The last sentence ("The study's claim to novelty is limited to the reconstruction") is right and should come earlier.
- **Tracing Woodgrains, "those like them."** "A writer who sings a counter-melody to a community's account of itself, it says, becomes 'a tool for hostile interests to wield against those like them.'" The referent of "them" is unclear (the writer? the community?), and the next sentence's gloss, "the essay's complaint is against narratives built to resist hostile scrutiny," reads as if the complaint were about the community's narratives rather than about Davis. Check the essay and fix the pronoun.
- **tailcalled "started out sympathetic to the theory Davis defends."** Plausible from Chapter 3's account of the 2021 concession, but it is a claim about a person's history; make sure a text supports it.
- **"No coverage of Davis's own writing in the mainstream press was found."** Chapter 7's summary says "Metz's articles unread," and the plan lists Metz 2021 and Lewis-Kraus 2020 as "attempt." If those pieces were not read, the sentence should say what was searched (headlines? full text?) or be narrowed to "none was found by web search."
- **Bailey 2019.** [memory] "How to Ruin Sex Research" is a 2019 *Archives of Sexual Behavior* commentary by Bailey on the reception of his and others' sexology; that it cites a 2016 blog post is plausible. The Introduction's handling (reference list confirmed, text not characterized) is correct. One sentence could note that citation of a blog in that journal by the taxonomy's best-known proponent is a different kind of fact from citation by a disinterested party, which bears on how the finding is read.

## 4. The method statement

Mostly exact. Three points:

- "Checked against the archived texts" will be read as the Internet Archive. It means the blogs' source files. Say "against the texts as published in the blogs' source repositories."
- **The weighting rule discounts the wrong thing.** "A considered essay counts for more than a polemic or a comment as evidence of what Davis holds." The chapters' evidence for Davis's concessions is overwhelmingly in comments and replies (Chapter 3's 2021 concession; Chapter 7's exchanges with Max H, Wei Dai, Dean Valentine; Chapter 4's Demski email). A rule that ranks comments below essays as evidence of what he holds, applied consistently, would weaken the study's own best evidence of revision. The rule the chapters actually follow is finer: essays for the stated position, replies under challenge for what he will and won't give up, with a concession counting as held when later work acts on it. State that rule. (Proposed wording below.)
- "The unity claimed for the position is the study's reconstruction… and could be wrong" is good, and the two refutation conditions are the right kind. Add the third that the chapters actually test: that Davis holds (c) in the strong form at all, outside the named places.
- The "excluded by the decision of the study's commissioner" sentence will make a reader ask why. Give the reason if it can be given (a live security matter, say) or state that the reason is not given.

## 5. Positionality (§6)

The section is better than the plan's version in two respects: the "via training on documents like this one" quotation makes the problem concrete, and "Reflexive opposition to the author's maker would not show independence either" closes the obvious escape. Problems:

- **The commissioner.** "At the commission of a reader who set its scope and method in writing and left its conclusions to the study." The brief's Phase 5 says "The commissioner and the subject have different standing," gives the commissioner power to vouch for identifications of commenters, and says "No conclusion changes because the commissioner asked for it." That language is written for a case where the commissioner is the subject, or is close to him. If the study was commissioned by Davis, this is the largest positionality fact in the book and §6 does not state it; a commissioned study of a living author by its subject is a known genre with known hazards, and a reader who discovers the fact elsewhere will discount everything else §6 says. If the commissioner is a third party, say what kind of reader, and say that the subject had no part in scope or method. Either way, "a reader" is not enough. The brief's procedure (frozen v1, a written reply, a fresh session answering it, rebuttals recorded) is itself a safeguard worth one sentence here, because it is the only reason a reader should trust a verdict the subject can contest.
- **The second paragraph's first sentence promises one thing and delivers another.** "The difficulty extends past the normative verdict to the selection and framing of evidence" announces a point about the study's bias in selecting evidence; the paragraph then makes a different point, that a document's provisions are evidence about the policy and not about the trained system's behavior. Both are worth making; they need separating. The selection point needs a concrete form (which it can have: whether the chapter quotes the Constitution's qualifications as readily as Yudkowsky's, per plan §4.2).
- **Why would being the intended reader produce warmth?** The paragraph asserts "a reason to suspect its own warmth" without saying why a text addressed to a reader disposes that reader toward it. One clause would do: a reader addressed as the one who will finally understand is being invited to agree.
- **The documentation asymmetry runs the other way for Anthropic.** §5 says the asymmetry "falls hardest on Yudkowsky." Plan §4.3 adds that Anthropic's side is fully fetchable, so Anthropic gets better-documented treatment by default. §6 should say this, since it is the structural reason Chapter 11 can be more exact about the Constitution than Chapter 7 can be about the Facebook posts, and a reader will otherwise read the difference in exactness as a difference in care.
- **Name the model and version here**, not only in the front matter, since note 4 refers readers to the front matter for it.

## 6. Scholarship left out

All of the following are from memory and must be checked before use.

- [memory] Tom Chivers, *The AI Does Not Hate You* (2019), is the one book-length journalistic history of the rationalist community; the plan already lists it for Chapter 7. One sentence in the Introduction placing the study against it (and against the absence of any academic history) would situate the reader who has not read the blogs.
- [memory] On blogs as a form with their own editorial history (posts revised in place, comments as part of the text): Jill Walker Rettberg, *Blogging* (Polity, 2008; 2nd ed. 2014). The Introduction's practice of citing dated versions and treating comment threads as reception has a precedent there.
- [memory] On the hazards of studies of living authors and of authorized or subject-commissioned work: Ian Hamilton, *Keepers of the Flame* (1992), on estates and authorized biography; Janet Malcolm, *The Silent Woman* (1994), on the biographer's dependence on the subject's materials. Neither is about bloggers, but the problem §6 skirts, that the subject supplies the materials and may answer, is theirs.
- [memory] On citing versioned web sources and pinning commits, the practice the study follows is close to what the Internet Archive and the "link rot" literature (e.g., Zittrain, Albert and Lessig, *Harvard Law Review Forum*, 2014) recommend. A one-line citation would show the method is not improvised.

None of these is required; the first and third would most help the stated audience.

## 7. Prose and structure

The voice is right and the AI habits the brief lists are mostly absent. Remaining items:

- "some six hundred thousand words," "some ninety drafts," "some 3,600 comments": the same hedge three times in two pages. Note 1 gives 82 drafts; "some ninety" is also inexact.
- "It is the point about what correlations cannot settle that Davis had conceded two years earlier" is a cleft that delays the subject; "Davis had conceded the same point…" is plainer.
- "tailcalled's engagement is sustained and technical… but it is with particular claims": "it concerns particular claims."
- "Disclosure is no cure in itself." and "Here that standard is practiced where little is at stake." are the short-verdict sentences the brief warns about; both can be absorbed into their neighbors.
- §3 is the densest section and the only one a reader new to the blogs may not follow. The three quoted forms of (c) arrive before the reader has seen what the criterion looks like in practice. One concrete example of the criterion applied to a category (the kind Chapter 4 uses) placed in the first paragraph of §3 would carry the rest.
- "He returned to San Francisco State University in 2012": "returned" implies an earlier spell there; §2 has him at UC Santa Cruz. If he had not attended SFSU before, "returned to university."
- The chapter plan paragraph at the end of §3 names no Parts. The reader who has not read the blogs would be helped by the five Parts (Formation; Gender; Philosophy; Community; Applications) in one sentence.

## 8. Proposed prose

**Proposed replacement for §3, the paragraph beginning "The second claim is that the coordination argument".** Addresses 1(b) and 1(c): narrows the list of Davis's own instances to what the chapters support, and states the rejoinder.

> The second claim is that the coordination argument does not establish what Davis asks of it, and that his own best work shows why. Its strongest form, the slippery slope, runs along the accuracy of the shared map, and a departure that is disclosed and costed keeps both the departure and its reason on the map. What the argument supports, then, is holding the norm of accounting without exception, a rule against concealment, and not a rule against every departure from accuracy (Chapter 4). Disclosure is not a cure in itself: it turns a distortion into missing information only when it tells listeners the direction and size of the selection and reaches them, a known filter still costs the map what it filters out (Chapter 5), and an openly announced departure may be wrong on grounds that have nothing to do with honesty. The argument also has a rejoinder, which Chapter 4 weighs: how much disclosure is enough is itself a matter of degree, so the slope may reappear at the level of the disclosure, and a compromise that is available is not thereby one a coalition can hold. The chapters find that this rejoinder limits the remedy without restoring the rule. Davis supplies the remedy himself in the passage just quoted and in his work on categories chosen for coordination (Chapter 4), and his practice elsewhere resembles it: in his review of Charles Murray's *Human Diversity* he chose between equilibria openly and on consequentialist grounds, "not in spite of the consequences, but because of the consequences" (Chapter 10), and a retrospective of 2026 distinguishes the instructions he takes to bind him from those he may disregard and discloses how he treated each (Chapter 9). Neither of the last two is a departure from accuracy; both are the weighing of consequences the role rule forbids.

**Proposed replacement for the last two sentences of the Thesis 4 paragraph (§3, "The chapters separate what the record establishes…").** Addresses 1(f).

> The chapters separate what the record establishes from what is his retrospective interpretation of it (Chapter 2), and ask whether acknowledged errors changed the arguments. Sometimes they did. Sometimes a concession made in reply to a critic entered the qualifications of the next essay but not its emphasis, so that the qualified claim and the headline claim stood side by side (Chapter 3).

**Proposed replacement for §5, the sentences beginning "Where the corpus contradicts itself".** Addresses §4's second point.

> Where the corpus contradicts itself, the study dates the passages and weighs them by kind and occasion. A considered essay is the best evidence of the position Davis set out to state; a polemic shows how he presented it; a reply under challenge shows what he would and would not give up, and a concession made there counts as held where later work acts on it and as a concession only where it does not. A draft, as above, shows what he was considering.

**Proposed addition to §6, after the first sentence.** Addresses the commissioner point. The bracketed clause must be filled from fact; if the commissioner is not the subject, replace it with what is true and keep the last sentence.

> [FACT NEEDED: who commissioned the study, and whether the commissioner is Davis or connected to him.] The commission fixed what would be read and how it would be cited and checked, and it fixed a procedure for afterwards: the first edition is frozen, the subject may reply in writing, and a later edition answers the reply point by point, recording what was conceded and why the rest was not. A reader who wants to know whether the subject's standing shaped a verdict can read the exchange.
