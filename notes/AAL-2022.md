### Comment on "Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society"
- Path: `AAL_content/2022/comment-on-propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society.md` · 2022-07-09 · philosophy (artificial intelligence) · 2373 w · https://zackmdavis.net/blog/2022/Jul/comment-on-propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society/
- Summary: LW-first. Bostrom & Shulman's list feels "too conservative, too anthropomorphic": rights/consent/"abused subordinate caste" framed for evolved creatures; we are the AIs' EEA, not a king needing a Magna Carta. Carrick Flynn 2028 propaganda-bot thought experiment: silencing might feel like suffocation; the problem is misalignment, not exploitation. Notes "align" appears 8 times vs. "nonhuman animals" 9; "successfully aligned AIs may be due compensation" implies a weaker sense of alignment — compensation is technology for getting along with unaligned agents. Cooperation in human society may rest on lack of power disparities; humans don't cooperate with weaker species — as AIs grow, "_we_ occupy the role of 'nonhuman animals.'" Enthusiastic about archiving decommissioned AIs and kinder deployment environments; 99.99%/0.01% compromise works even with a paperclipper — "If only there were some way to actually, knowably make that deal."
- Themes: ai-welfare, alignment-strong-sense, anthropomorphism, bostrom-shulman, game-theory-of-power, cosmic-compromise
- Xrefs: AAL 2023–24 AI posts; 2026 corrigibility/constitution posts.
- Quotes:
> But with subhuman AIs, we're _not_ in the position of a potentially tyrannical king who needs to be restrained by a Magna Carta enumerating his subjects' rights; a better metaphor would be to say that we're in the position of the AIs' _environment of evolutionary adaptedness_.
> the concept of "owing compensation" (whether for complying with restrictions, or for conferring benefits) is a social technology for getting along with _unaligned_ agents, who don't want exactly the same things as you.
> as digital minds become more powerful than us, _we_ occupy the role of "nonhuman animals." How's that going to turn out?
> If only—if only there were some way to actually, knowably make that deal, and not just write philosophy papers about it.
