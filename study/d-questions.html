Apparatus DQuestions for the Author

The study's conclusions rest on the texts. The questions below are ones the texts raise and only Davis could answer: matters of fact the corpus leaves unrecorded, and points on which his published replies stop short. Each is tied to the chapter that raises it. None is a condition of that chapter's conclusions, and any answer would be weighed as the Introduction (§6) describes.

  1. The figure in Yudkowsky's 2016 post. Davis's published quotations of Yudkowsky's Facebook post of March 2016 give its estimate as at least 20% of the ones with penises are actually women ("Blanchard's Dangerous Idea and the Plight of the Lucid Crossdreamer," UUT, 8 Jul 2023). An unpublished sequel to the memoir asks whether Sabbatai Zevi, in Yudkowsky's position, would also declare that 30% of the ones with penises are actually women (unpublished draft, "Guess I'll Die"), and in a recorded conversation of 24 June 2025 Davis told Cade Metz that I am obviously in that 30% ("Conversations With Cade Metz on the Rationalists," LW, 1 Jul 2026). Is "30%" a slip of memory, and does anything in Davis's account of the post depend on the larger figure? (Chapter 7)
  2. The order of onset. "Sexual Dimorphism in Yudkowsky's Sequences" (2021) holds that the erotic fantasy came before the non-sexual feeling of being female that Davis calls the self-identity thing. Neither the diary entry of 2005 nor the notebook of 2006 records that feeling or dates it. Does any document earlier than 2021 do so? (Chapter 2)
  3. What would count against the negative claim. Davis's stated empirical thesis is that late-onset gender dysphoria in males is not an intersex condition, or at most, a very mild one ("The Categories Were Made for Man to Make Predictions," UUT, 23 Feb 2018). What finding about the brains or psychology of non-androphilic trans women would count as more than mild? (Chapter 3)
  4. The rival model. "You Are Right and I Was Wrong" (2021) calls Veale, Clarke and Lomax's identity-defense model of gender-variant development a stretch. On what grounds? (Chapter 3)
  5. The SEXNET message. Was the draft message of February 2025 on the sampling in Hsu, Morandini and Rudd's study sent to the list, and if so, did its authors reply? (Chapter 3)
  6. Goals achieved by agreement. In January 2024 Wei Dai suggested that some goals people pursue through words, such as being treated with care by friends, are achieved by the friends' agreement itself, so that a word's use can change the territory it describes. The thread records no reply. How does Davis's account of categories treat such goals? (Chapter 4)
  7. The ally test. The draft "Accidentally Misleading People Should Be Embarrassing" (July 2026) proposes asking whether one would have read a rival's conduct the same way had it been an ally's. Would Davis have read Kelsey Piper's warning to Richard Ngo, which "Contra Piper on When Conversation Is Possible" (September 2026) reads as a veiled negotiating threat, the same way had it come from an ally? (Chapter 5)
  8. Salamon's disclosure. Davis's coin-room norm punishes selective reporting only when the selection criteria are not made clear. Does he hold that Anna Salamon's stated practice of accusing less than she would if accusation were costless shows that she does not believe in a norm of truth-telling, or only that her statement of the practice did not tell readers enough about what she withholds? (Chapter 6)
  9. The grounds withheld. The last published installment of the memoir closes with a judgment about Yudkowsky, prefaced by the statement that it discloses no content from private conversations that may or may not have happened. Without disclosing any such content, can Davis say what kind of evidence the judgment rests on? (Chapter 7)
  10. The other dialogues. In January 2026 Davis said that in the third of his dialogues on artificial intelligence Simplicia represents his own views. Is the same true of the first two? (Chapter 8)
  11. The false form. Davis's 2023 draft explains a hospital form's false statement that he had presented himself voluntarily as probably copied from an earlier note. Is that explanation his inference from the notes, or was it confirmed? (Chapter 9)
  12. Credence on the gap. The review of Murray's Human Diversity (2020) judged a genetic contribution to the American black–white gap in tested IQ probably part of the explanation and disclaimed expertise; "Agreeing With Stalin in Ways That Exhibit Generally Rationalist Principles" (2024) called Murray's position obviously correct. Did Davis's credence change between the two, and if so on what evidence? (Chapter 10)
  13. Equal treatment without equal belief. The unpublished review of Murray's Facing Reality (2021) asks whether a rule of equal treatment before the law can keep its authority once people no longer believe that it will lead to equal outcomes, and answers that Davis does not know. Does he now have an answer, and does he accept such a rule for the state while leaving belief free? (Chapter 10)
  14. Open refusal. Under "Terrified Comments on Corrigibility in Claude's Constitution" (2026) the commenter comex argued that room for a model to refuse openly may reduce the pressure toward covert subversion. The thread records no reply. What is Davis's answer? (Chapter 11)
