6. Pronouns, compression, and the late synthesis (2022–2025)

After 2021 the categorization writing changes register. The LessWrong posts had argued in the abstract; the four pieces treated here apply the finished theory to Yudkowsky directly, and two of them are the clearest statements of the mature view.

"Challenges to Yudkowsky's Pronoun Reform Proposal"

In February 2021 Yudkowsky posted on Facebook that the lack of a gender-neutral singular pronoun is a design flaw in English and that the simplest and best protocol is, "'He' refers to the set of people who have asked us to use 'he', with a default for those-who-haven't-asked that goes by gamete size" and to say that this just is the normative definition.Quoted in "Challenges to Yudkowsky's Pronoun Reform Proposal" (UUT, 13 March 2022), http://unremediatedgender.space/2022/Mar/challenges-to-yudkowskys-pronoun-reform-proposal/. The Facebook post is login-walled and is quoted only as the corpus quotes it. Davis's twelve-thousand-word reply, the longest thing in the categorization corpus after the memoirs, makes one argument from several angles. The proposal is self-undermining, because if it were actually true that he simply referred to those who take the pronoun he, then there would be no reason for trans people to care which pronoun people used for them. People ask for "she" because, in English, those are the pronouns for males and females respectively; misgendering is received as an attack, not as a parsing error. "Pronouns shouldn't convey sex-category information" is a fine motte for a bailey that does not follow from it. The claim that Yudkowsky cannot feel a pronoun attached to anything in his head "more firmly than 'doesn't look like an Oliver'" is met with a joke about a man named Oliver whose face is shown; if Yudkowsky gets the joke, Davis argues, his brain has done the thing he says it does not do.

The post's most important move for this chapter is a distinction it draws against both Yudkowsky and the crude version of its own side: pronouns can have meaningful semantics without being denotative statements that can be straightforwardly "false", and the people Yudkowsky is arguing with are largely not claiming that "pronouns are lies". That is right, and it should be read back onto "Unnatural Categories": if a pronoun is not a proposition, then the equivalence between a category system and a lying policy, which was proved for declarative signals, does not apply to it directly. What applies is the weaker claim that a pronoun convention conveys sex-category information and that a convention which detaches the pronoun from the category imposes a cost on everyone whose head still has them attached. Davis's own case is the example: asking people to call him "she" would be an obviously incredibly bad decision, because anyone who looks at me can see at a glance that I'm male, and the cost would be this tremendous awkwardness and fakeness suffusing all future social interactions involving me. The conclusion is not that Yudkowsky's policy is wrong but that it is not neutral: sometimes there are cases where there is no "neutral" policy, because all available policies impose costs on someone and there's no solution that everyone is happy with.

This is the coordination theory of section 4 again, now applied with care, and it is a strong post. The LessWrong reception is instructive. Oliver Habryka, who runs the site, wrote that basically everything in this post strikes me as "obviously true" and that Yudkowsky's original post had shifted his sense of the community from trusting its leadership to maintain epistemic coherence under pressure to expecting that depending on the circumstances community leaders might make up sophisticated stories for why pretty obviously true things are false in order to not have to deal with complicated political issues.habryka, comment of 13 March 2022, 104 karma, LessWrong. gjm, who had criticized "Blegg Mode" in 2019, wrote that Zack is clever and insightful and I agree with most of the specific things he says, but that pretty much all he ever does here is to point out errors made in support of one particular side of a live sociopolitical debate, and that it's possible for one's thinking to be made worse overall by a process of correcting errors, if the choice of which errors has enough bias in it.gjm, comment of 14 March 2022, LessWrong. Both are fair. The post is right about what it says and selective in what it chooses to say it about, and Davis, in the 2025 piece discussed below, would concede the selection and defend it.

"Beyond the Binary"

"Beyond the Binary" (December 2023) is the mature theory stated in three thousand words, and it is the post a reader with limited time should read."Beyond the Binary" (UUT, 12 December 2023), http://unremediatedgender.space/2023/Dec/beyond-the-binary/. It begins with an invented puzzle (a human whose gonads produce eggs but who appears male in every other respect: female or male?) and refuses it: such a person does not exist, and the cognitive technology of "words" having "definitions" doesn't work in the world of the imagination. Then the bitstring model. In a world where every twenty-bit string is equally likely, no five-bit codeword can compress anything; language is possible only because the distribution is non-uniform, because almost all the strings one sees are near all-zeros or all-ones. A definition is a pointer: Discrete features make for better word definitions than high-dimensional statistical regularities, even if most of the everyday inferential utility of using the word comes from the high-dimensional statistical correlates. Gametes, genitals, and chromosomes are the never-flipped first bit: It's not that people directly care about gametes or chromosomes or even genitals in most everyday situations. It is that they are discrete features that are causally entangled with everything else that differs between females and males, including differences that haven't even been discovered yet. And then the admission that the previous section has been building toward: because our brains are good at using sex-category words to simultaneously encode predictions about both absolute discrete differences and high-dimensional statistical regularities of various effect sizes, without our being consciously aware of the cognitive work being done, it's easy to get confused by verbal gymnastics if you don't know the theory.

The post is even-handed in a way the LessWrong material rarely was. It says that the gender-critical slogan "sex is just gametes" isn't going to fly either, because people who defend single-sex spaces too want mere statistical tendencies to be part of the concept; it grants that the stereotype complaint against trans ideology has something to it because removing the discrete anchors leaves nothing but statistical tendencies; and it bites the bullet it has been avoiding since 2018, that on this view masculine women and feminine men are in some sense less "real" women and men, respectively. It even offers the olive branch Ozy had offered in 2018, in Davis's own idiom: maybe we should talk about categories like "masculine people" (including both typical men, and butch lesbians) more often! The one sentence I would press on is the hinge: the typical trans advocate shell game of just replacing "sex" with "gender" and letting people choose their "gender" isn't going to fly, because sex actually exists and we have a need for language to talk about it. Both halves of the "because" are true. Neither entails that the short word must be the one reserved for sex. The post's own theory, that definitions are pointers to natural abstractions and that the everyday utility comes from the correlates, would be equally satisfied by a convention in which "woman" points to the perceived-sex abstraction and "female" to the developmental one, which is roughly the convention Davis's interlocutors have been proposing. He has shown that there must be a word for sex. He has not shown which word.

"Prediction Markets Are Not a Drop-In Replacement for Concepts"

The 2024 post takes its text from Yudkowsky's fiction, in which a character suggests that a military with prediction markets would not need to consult sex, since If there's more information to be gained from measuring biological sex than by just measuring height, it'll show up in the military prediction markets."Prediction Markets Are Not a Drop-In Replacement for Concepts" (UUT, 21 December 2024), http://unremediatedgender.space/2024/Dec/prediction-markets-are-not-a-drop-in-replacement-for-concepts/, quoting Yudkowsky and Lintamande's Planecrash. Davis's reply is a short, clean piece of decision theory. A market can only match a decision procedure if its traders are free to run that procedure, so the market's existence is no argument against the procedure; It would be lying to claim that Society doesn't need to compute P because it has prediction markets, if, "under the hood", the traders in the market are in fact computing P all the time. A restaurant-choice market does not abolish the concept of Italian food, because the first thing a trader will do is search for Italian restaurants, not because they're fools who think that "Italian food" is somehow ontologically fundamental and eternal, but because there contingently do happen to be approximate conditional independence relationships between the features of meals served by different restaurants. And the real question is institutional: whether institutions should be able to take category membership into account as a matter of explicit policy (rather than only implicitly via the black box of a prediction market, whose traders are allowed to notice things that the policy isn't). The argument is correct and the point generalizes: "we have a better predictor, so we don't need the concept" is a fallacy whenever the predictor uses the concept. The post is also a good example of the deniable-allegory method working as intended, since it can be read in full without any reference to gender and is, in that reading, simply right. I note, for the record of reception, that the post ends by reading the fiction's text closely to decide whether its author endorses the character's view, and concludes that he does; this is literary criticism of a kind the chapter cannot assess, since I have not read the novel.

"A Generic Ontological Finickiness"

The last post in the sequence (2025) answers a Yudkowsky thread arguing that outrage at transition is tied to what a culture thinks is being stolen, not to generic ontological finickiness, and is therefore not a philosophical position at all."A Generic Ontological Finickiness" (UUT, 14 September 2025), http://unremediatedgender.space/2025/Sep/a-generic-ontological-finickiness/. The quoted Yudkowsky thread is on X and is quoted only as the corpus quotes it. Davis's answer is the best short defense of the whole program's motive. People who point out that pyrite is not gold are still telling the truth even if their motivation for speaking up is that they think gold is valuable and pyrite is not, rather than a "generic ontological finickiness." He concedes the selectivity gjm had complained of (I selfishly focus on the topics that are important to me.) and claims the generality at the level of method: there are general laws of cognition governing language and thought, which I seek to understand and apply in my work in the hopes of improving the accuracy of shared maps. The methodology is general even if the choice of topic is idiosyncratic and personal. He also, in a footnote, states the inferential claim in the form the sexology chapter will have to assess: what it means to say that 6'2" women "are still women", is that we can confidently predict they'll be more female-typical on average among the innumerable other measurable properties of a person.

Set the 2025 post beside the 2018 one and the shape of the development is clear. The empirical criterion for category membership (cheap features that predict decision-relevant ones) has been replaced by a causal one (a discrete root variable that buys conditional independence across everything downstream). The claim that redefinition is "deception" has been restated as the claim that one can tell the truth selectively. The hostility to "social construction" has been replaced by a theory of Schelling categories on which social constructions are real coordination equilibria with costs. What has not changed is the insistence that a particular word belongs to a particular cluster, and that is the one claim the theory never supported.
