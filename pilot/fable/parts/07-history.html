7. The Category War as history

The three memoir posts of 2023–24 ("A Hill of Validity in Defense of Meaning," "If Clarity Seems Like Death to Them," "Agreeing With Stalin in Ways That Exhibit Generally Rationalist Principles") narrate the dispute as Davis experienced it, and they are the fullest account of the Category War there is. They are also one side's account, written five years later with the correspondence to hand; the other parties have not published theirs, and most of the primary documents (Yudkowsky's tweets, his Facebook posts, the emails) are either login-walled or private. What follows takes the memoirs as evidence of Davis's reasoning and conduct, which they are, and treats their claims about other people's motives as claims."A Hill of Validity in Defense of Meaning" (UUT, 15 July 2023), http://unremediatedgender.space/2023/Jul/a-hill-of-validity-in-defense-of-meaning/; "If Clarity Seems Like Death to Them" (UUT, 30 December 2023), http://unremediatedgender.space/2023/Dec/if-clarity-seems-like-death-to-them/; "Agreeing With Stalin…" (UUT, 2 March 2024). The quoted tweets and Facebook posts are cited only as the corpus quotes them; see the limitations note in the preface.

What happened

On 28 November 2018 Yudkowsky posted a Twitter thread which, as Davis quotes it, began: Some people I usually respect for their willingness to publicly die on a hill of facts, now seem to be talking as if pronouns are facts, or as if who uses what bathroom is necessarily a factual statement about chromosomes. Come on, you know the distinction better than that! Later tweets in the thread said that "Using language in a way you dislike, openly and explicitly and with public focus on the language and its meaning, is not lying" and that "you're not standing in defense of truth if you insist on a word, brought explicitly into question, being used with some particular meaning." Davis read this as a reversal of "37 Ways That Words Can Be Wrong," and the memoir is candid about the magnitude of his reaction: if the rationalists were going to get the philosophy of language wrong and Eliezer Yudkowsky was in on it (!!!), that was intolerable, inexplicable, incomprehensible—like there wasn't a real world anymore. He emailed Yudkowsky with an offer to pay a "cheerful price" to have his post read; he and a small group of allies wrote again, and again, and a fourth time after "Where to Draw the Boundaries?" was published in April 2019. He explains the dolphin post's indirection with a line from Schelling about helping one's counterparty make a concession: By making the post be about dolphins, I was trying to deny myself too great of a reward on the gender-politics front. In December 2019 he wrote to Alexander asking for an explicit and loud clarification or partial retraction of the 2014 essay; Alexander's editorial note followed within the month. In September 2020, after a further angry email that the memoir reproduces at length, Yudkowsky published a Facebook post that, as Davis reads it, restricted his 2018 criticism to naive essentialism and contained the line you are being the bad guy if you try to shut down that conversation by saying that "I can define the word 'woman' any way I want". Davis: There it is! A clear ex cathedra statement that gender categories are not an exception to the general rule that categories aren't arbitrary. He emailed his allies a thank-you note with the subject line "the end of the Category War," and wrote, in retrospect, At the cost of two years of my life, we finally got a clarification from Yudkowsky that you can't define the word woman any way you like.

Yudkowsky's February 2021 pronoun post reopened it, and the dolphin tweets of June 2021 reopened it again. By 2024 the memoir's judgment had hardened into a conditional charge: if Yudkowsky can't unambiguously choose Truth over Feelings, then he's a fraud. Davis insists this is meant in Merriam-Webster's sense, "a person who is not what he or she pretends to be", applies Yudkowsky's own words about Stephen Jay Gould ("[c]onsistently self-serving scientific 'error', in the face of repeated correction and without informing others of the criticism, blends over into scientific fraud."), and reserves evaluation as a separate step. Of Alexander he says only: Scott Alexander chose Feelings, but I can't hold that against him, because Scott is explicit about only speaking in the capacity of some guy with a blog.

What it shows

Three things stand out when the story is set beside the philosophy.

First, the narrow point was conceded, and the memoirs say so. Alexander's note says that one should "definitely worry about" definitions and that plausible ones are limited to a few possibilities suggested by the territory. Yudkowsky's clarification says that "I can define 'woman' any way I want" is the bad guy's move. Those are the propositions "Where to Draw the Boundaries?" was written to establish, and by Davis's own reading they were established. What did not change was anyone's policy: Alexander still used trans people's preferred pronouns, Yudkowsky still proposed self-chosen pronouns with a gamete-size default, and so, Davis says more than once, does Davis. On the account of the dispute given in section 5(d), that is the expected outcome. If the question of the word's default sense is a coordination problem between two natural categories, then establishing that categories are not arbitrary leaves the coordination problem exactly where it was. The war was won and nothing moved because the thing won was not the thing contested.

Second, the memoir shows the apparatus of the program being applied with a strictness that the program itself does not support. The "fraud" charge rests on the equivalence of "Unnatural Categories": a speaker who adopts a boundary for its effects is, functionally, lying, and a speaker who does so while claiming epistemic heroism is a fraud. But section 5(b) argued, from Davis's own concessions to Demski and Martínez, that the equivalence holds only relative to a cooperative baseline and that an openly declared convention is not deception in the sense that matters. Yudkowsky's pronoun proposal is openly declared. One can think it wrong, as Davis does and as I do in part, without its being a lie; and the move from "wrong" to "fraud" is the move that the philosophy, read carefully, forbids. Davis's allies pushed him toward the stronger word, and the memoir records his own resistance to it: I still sympathized with the pushback from Caliphate supporters against using "scam"/"fraud"/"lie"/&c. language to include motivated elephant-in-the-brain-like distortions. By 2024 the resistance had gone.

Third, the method. The cheerful prices, the group emails, the stealth posts, and the memoir's reconstruction of private correspondence are all defended in the text on decision-theoretic grounds, and the defenses are not frivolous. But the record also contains the costs. A commenter told Davis's collaborator that by copying the group on the emails he had "sacrificed all hope of success in favor of maintaining his own sanity by CC'ing you guys." Anna Salamon asked him to stop sending cheerful-price offers. The dolphin thread ended in an apology. And Michael Vassar, the ally who pushed hardest, eventually told Davis that the philosophy articulated in "A Human's Guide to Words" was inadequate for politicized environments where our choice of ontology is constrained: that without a third gender or a public fluent in cluster-talk, the available ways to think about trans women were creepy men (the TERF narrative), crazy men (the medical model), or a protected class of actual woman. That is the coordination objection of section 5(a), from inside the posse, and the memoir reports it without answering it.

Reception of the memoirs

The LessWrong reception of the memoirs was large and mostly serious. The moderator Raemon, responding to Davis's summary of what he had wanted, wrote that he agreed Alexander's sentence was false and Scott should retract it and we should all agree he should retract it, and that "it's important to preserve a category for 'actual women' that you can still talk about." But he was still unsure whether you think this court ruling is about "on LessWrong / in truth-focused contexts/communities", "worldwide in all contexts", and whether the demand was that rationalists refuse a social category of women that includes unsuccessfully transitioned trans women because of reasoning like "there are rules of rationality suggesting this has epistemic costs, but the social benefits outweigh those costs."Raemon, comment of 17 July 2023, LessWrong. That is the question this chapter has been asking, and the fact that a sympathetic reader could not find the answer in a fifty-thousand-word memoir is itself a finding.

Vaniver's summary for a baffled commenter is the most balanced account of the affair by a third party that I found. The community's implicit promises were about core epistemic virtues and discussion practices; Davis saw Eliezer as breaking a lot of these core expectations, not being open about it or acknowledging it when being called out, and also others not reacting to Eliezer breaking those expectations. But the promises were (I think) clearly aspirational / mediated by the pressures of having to actually exist in the world. I do think it makes sense to have a heresy budget, and I think Zack got unlucky with the obsession lottery. And the sentence that best states what went wrong: Instead they made philosophical arguments that, as far as I can tell, were not correct, and this was crazy-making.Vaniver, comment of 31 December 2023, 53 karma, LessWrong. I think Vaniver is right on every point, including the one that cuts against Davis. A community that had said "we are being greengrocers on this issue, sorry" would have been conceding a coordination constraint, which is what Davis's own theory says the constraint is. What it did instead was produce bad philosophy to avoid saying that, and Davis was right to object to the bad philosophy, and wrong to conclude that the thing being protected was a falsehood rather than a convention.

Hostile reception exists and should be recorded. On the subreddit SneerClub in 2023, a commenter who read "The Categories Were Made for Man to Make Predictions" wrote that an analysis which questions whether trans women form a homogeneous category without questioning whether 'cis women' makes sense as a category is biased, and that this makes the idea of taking this writer seriously as a thinker on gender a nonstarter.r/SneerClub thread of 7 February 2023, read in the archived text in reception_data/web/. That objection is answered by the 2021 and 2023 posts (the cis category is "questioned" at length, and found to be rooted at a discrete variable), but it was made against the 2018 essay, which does take the female cluster for granted. The rest of the thread is abuse and does not engage the arguments.
