Anecdotally some now-radfems were fine in queer spaces even at the point in time when there was a shallow norm that you should talk in gender(-identity) terms without the deeper norm that you should write sex or perceived sex or the first half of someone's life out of your understanding of the world. (At least in my circles people were pretty fine circa 2010 with for example, acting like being a trans man is a lesbian trajectory as long as you didn't quite say it.) At some point early last decade (maybe 2012-2013) there was e.g., tons of pushback from trans women about trans-inclusive queer women's spaces also including trans men, because that 'implied' (as in revealed) trans women were being included as a third gender category and not as women and that people still had some affinities along sex lines. The dating preferences thing is nearly a cheap shot at this point, but it's also probably the most common reason lesbians get disillusioned with queer politics. Five years ago I think it would have been easy to win lesbians back just by: 1) accepting that the vast majority of lesbians think of their attraction in sex terms even if they might date passing trans women, and 2) not [deriding](http://juliaserano.blogspot.com/2013/03/faab-mentality.html) people for suggesting AFAB people have something in common. The longer it gets though, the easier it is to think of those things as near-inevitable outcome of earlier norms; it's possible to recognize the conflicts as kind of narrow and still not resolvable, even if some might not put it that explicitly. (The set of GC people who learned about most of these conflicts online is a distinct group I don't have a lot of insight into, but I don't think they significantly have the same priorities.)

I don't think the 'believe-it-but-don't-say-it' thing is a stable norm after witnessing the last decade, but I think plenty of younger lesbian radfems are not super-aligned with straight radfems and would be back in queer communities the second (to be a little rude) that trans women stop being weird. Some who do want (afab) lesbian-only spaces see trans people as having overlapping concerns, even if they (we) resent being asked to take those concerns up as their own. I don't blame people who think that ship has sailed though -- to some extent the language stuff is a power play and it's easier to 'win' by drawing a hard line. I'm also not actually sure that the radfems who insist that you are not going to include male people without their priorities... being prioritized are wrong.

As someone with a decade of lesbian community participation under my belt (as an autoandrophilic AFAB butch dyke, if credentials are needed) I'm finding Lease Bound a conceptually fascinating read in that it appears to take place in a wholly imaginary, presumably aspirational version of a dyke subculture - particularly noteworthy is the total absence of femme expression, prior to the respective intrusions of Blaire the bisexual trans propagandist/Halsey lookalike and the hook-nosed "transbian" deviant trio, in an urban Australian dyke bar milieu! The lesbian bar gang (+ Riley) have a grade-school-math-textbook type of diversity to them and if nothing else certainly look the part of stylish urban butches yet seem devoid of the sexuality inherent to most real-life displayers of such expressions - they would sooner show up to Club Yonique with a baby in a sling than a strap-on in their boxers, if you will forgive a bit of crudeness. It's common for trans-exclusionary lesbian rhetoric to be perceived (and sometimes expressed) as a kind of hankering for the past but to me comics such as Lease Bound make it clear that, as is so often the case, the hankering is not for a factual past but for a construct - in this case one built upon a sort of lesbian purity ideal, free of "male" influence through makeup and sexy outfits and penises and surgery but also free of "male" influence through overt female masculinity. It is not difficult (though fortunately far more difficult than self-identified TERFs would like) to find an AFAB-only lesbian community in real life, if you're willing to accept the coexistence of "he/they" pronoun badges, shaved heads, women in corsets who do burlesque in their spare time, and the women with shaved heads occasionally engaging in welcome locker-room-esque talk about said women in corsets. The lack of enthusiastic TERF presence in such "female-only" (in practice if not in theory) spaces makes me wonder if the Lease Bound audience isn't yearning for something that goes beyond simple "return to lesbian tradition", though I can't say I know how to identify exactly what that might be.
