@Lux & teapot, re squaring with Blanchardianism: There's the question of what we mean by Blanchardianism. One notion would be "truth-seeking sexology". People like Michael Bailey pretend to care about the true etiology of gender identity, sexual identities, etc., and they are right in claiming that if you want to do this, you're going to need concepts like AGP etc.. I don't think it's wrong to try to come up with truth-seeking sexological models to explain cases like this (i.e. to square this case with "Blanchardianism"), though I guess we don't seem to have enough information to do it this time. This doesn't mean that one should blindly follow typologies, and indeed there is a lot of classical Blanchardian theory that needs to be thrown out because it doesn't align with empirical facts.

And mainstream Blanchardians like Bailey and Brown have made quite clear that throwing that out is unacceptable to them, which then proves that Blanchardianism is not "truth-seeking sexology". Now we can have a spinoff, neo-Blanchardianism, which does attempt to be truth-seeking sexology, but it is really really expensive to run, so it's gonna die.

If Blanchardianism is not truth-seeking sexology, then what is it? This is a complicated question that I don't think I understand. My best bet is that it's a coalition of different people in the gay/trans space who have somewhat correlated perspectives. There's various people who've been impacted by disruptive transsexuality yet still want to support practical transsexuals, e.g. HSTSs who have to deal with chasers transitioning but who still want to support themselves. There's people like Bailey, who as far as I can tell has a dominant agenda of supporting unfairly marginalized theories. There's AGP repressors who want a different story than "you're supposed to transition". Etc.. Blanchardianism seems to be a set of ideas that works for their perspectives, such that they're not really interested in looking further.

Squaring this case with the coalitional version of Blanchardianism would, AFAICT, require coming up with a set of memes that they find useful in their day-to-day dealings with autogynephiles and disruptive transsexuals, and which gives space for the gay2lesbian trans women. That is not an endeavor I feel like bothering with, though; realistically there is not going to be a truth-seeking etiological answer to it, because there is a strong sparsity preference in the theories due to it being burdensome to deal with too complex ideas, and this sparsity preference is not compatible with truth-seeking about etiologies as reality has a surprising amount of detail.
