Is "sex is immutable and determined by the presence of a Y chromosome, no exceptions" actually a stable Schelling point? I feel like most people see it as absurd to treat people with CAIS as men since their bodies are externally pretty indistinguishable from an ordinary female one, especially since a lot of them don't even know themselves they have it; you can't require karyotyping before entering every public restroom. I don't think many people actually alieve very-well-passing trans people are their AGAB either; consider the case of Ben Shapiro repeatedly gendering Laverne Cox correctly and 'correcting' himself to male pronouns. So I don't think a system that treats them as their AGAB in every context would be very stable, people would have a natural inclination to not follow it in some contexts. Of course, by the same criteria, self-identity wouldn't be a totally stable Schelling point either, as most people do not find it easy to consistently use pronouns not according with a person's apparent sex. (But maybe they would if they had more experience doing so; it seems to come easier to people who hang out around a lot of trans and non-binary people.)

Anecdotally most people seem to find it somewhat easy to 'move the line' by classifying by gender expression rather than sex, but not to just use what someone wants. Someone who uses 'he' for trans men also often instinctively uses 'he' for butch women.  This can be a learned line--e.g., do I live somewhere where top surgery is a reliable sign of how someone identifies--but it doesn't seem like many people instinctively remember individual self-id as much as they learn a vibes-based classification rather than a percieved-sex-based one.

Personally I find it easier for trans women because there's less overlap in presentation between cis men who present similarly to trans women out of drag--at least as someone who does not really hang out in gay male spaces--than there is between trans men and butch lesbians.
