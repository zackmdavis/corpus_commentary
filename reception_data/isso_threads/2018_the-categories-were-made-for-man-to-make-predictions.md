Well, I guess where I'm going with this is that trans women in the West tend to be bi or gynephilic at much higher rates than cis women.

If I understand Lawrence's argument correctly, the split between androphilic HSTS trans women and bi/gynephilic AGP trans women wouldn't be consistent from culture to culture - it would vary based on how collectivist or individualist the culture was. In other words, (and I'm basically paraphrasing here) AGP males wouldn't transition in a collectivist culture - only HSTS males would. So surveying trans women's orientation cross-culturally could confirm or fail to confirm AL's ideas.

The two studies I cited do survey trans women's orientation, though each uses a different way to frame the data (& Skorska's paper doesn't have it as a main focus.) Thai transfems ("sao praphet song") are very heavily exclusively androphilic. European trans women not so much.

So what I'm trying to say here is that it looks like AL's predicted split across culture in trans women's orientation, that she claimed to have found in two pieces that she wrote several years ago, has shown up in papers by two different teams published 2021.

Hope that makes sense. Please feel free to poke holes in my ideas :P

Blanchardians have a habit of interpreting any correlation with the types as supportive of the typology. But of course it's only supportive of the typology if the correlation is better derived from the typology than without it.
