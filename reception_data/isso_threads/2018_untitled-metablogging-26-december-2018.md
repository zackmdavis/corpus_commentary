I think one challenge you're going to get is when you lump different groups together. Unusually GNC people and A*Ps struggling with transition could plausibly both benefit from a nonbinary gender, but could they benefit from the same nonbinary gender? It seems to me like the nonbinary gender they would need would differ.

But I also came to think about MTSW's counterargument:

> 'But I'm Not Quite Sure What That Means': Costs of Nonbinary Gender as a Social Technology

It might be a bit premature to respond already. 😅 But I'm going to do it anyway: Before starting to write this comment, I thought that "But I'm Not Quite Sure What That Means" would be a quite relevant argument against nonbinary gender. But upon reflection, I'm not really sure this is so. Usually people don't really have social technologies explained to them, instead they just get used to them. Nonbinary gender is odd because it's a new social technology that most people don't have experience with, and really a lot of the specific norms are still getting worked out.

But as the norms become more consistent and as people gain more experience either by interacting with NB people or by being exposed to NB people in the media, they will presumably end up with some sense for how to handle it and what to do in practice. Similar to how people can navigate all sorts of other social situations without knowing Bayes or game theory or anything like that. We're going through an expensive transition period where people are both defining and learning the concept, but after that period I bet it's going to work about as seamlessly as any other social technology.
