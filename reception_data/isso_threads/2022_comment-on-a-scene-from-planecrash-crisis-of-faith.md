#### [>] tailcalled · 0 likes · 2024-09-23 · http://unremediatedgender.space/2022/Jun/comment-on-a-scene-from-planecrash-crisis-of-faith/#isso-584
Disclaimer: I know nothing about the setting of planecrash so my comment will be uninformed about setting-relevant factors, instead focused more on what I know of ~earth.

> And after you've added in prediction markets to actually get all of the information that anybody has, what remains to be gained by creating a law that asymmetrically treats different sapient beings in a way based not on predicted outcomes?

A lot? Much of it efficiency: not needing to fund prediction markets (prediction markets mainly shine in coming to consensus under adversarial circumstance, but if you have an adversarial relationship to those hiring personnel, you've already failed), not needing to holistically measure performance, etc. For complex multidimensional stuff like this, it tends to be much easier to measure inputs like sex than outputs like performance - almost no processes attempt to fully measure performance, even capitalism rewards companies on profit rather than contribution to societal welfare.

(Could one just make a performance test to make it simpler, like a sequence of exercises? Maybe with a lot of work but not really otherwise; a test can only really measure a weighted aggregate of contributing factors, but real-world performance is long-tailed, and typically different contributing factors lead to contributing with qualitatively different tails. You could try to have multiple tests corresponding to multiple different long-tailed factors, but at that point you're basically going to end up with separate performance criteria for males and females (or non-predicted-outcome predispositions) anyway.

Like for complex nonconvex optimization, you don't really go straight to predicting aggregate outcomes and then disaggregate. Rather, you start with the virtues/opportunities in your assignment and then narrow in on possible classes of outcomes, only ending up with final predictions once you've done a lot of discrimination.

If you don't do it this way, your ranking criteria will have a strongly homogenizing influence, because you will tend to rank based on how well people do at the most common assignments, or how well people do at the most important assignments of the most common kinds of people, so you cannot recognize people who will do unusually well based on assignments that are unique to them.)

Other than efficiency, prediction markets also have downsides in privacy, perverse incentives, being poorly able to deal with end-of-the-the-world scenarios (which ideally the military should be good at dealing with), and probably more that I'm forgetting.
