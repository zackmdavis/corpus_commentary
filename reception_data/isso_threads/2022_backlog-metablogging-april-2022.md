> a post about the motivation for positing peseudobisexuality or meta-attraction

I think meta-attraction exists, but I also think Blanchardians are careless in indiscriminately applying it for AGPs even though there appear to be some classically androphilic AGPs.

> correlation of homosexuality with childhood behavior makes it look like a prenatal organizational effect, rather than an activational effect that would respond to HRT as an adult.

I've actually been wondering recently about whether this type of argument really holds.

Clearly there is a strong incentive for evolution to have sexual orientation align with sex. So this makes evolution create mechanisms that "canalize" it, leading to people being consistently heterosexual.

But is there just one mechanism for doing that? I've seen biologists joke about how biology is full of parallel mechanisms that achieve the same goal. And many parallel mechanisms makes intuitive sense from my understanding of how evolution works, since evolution happens in parallel with tons of small effects adding up over many generations of large populations.

But if there are multiple parallel mechanisms, couldn't there be some that are dependent on activational hormonal effects, and some that are organizational? And then when these mechanisms come into conflict, unpredictable things would happen?

I don't know, it seems plausible that adult hormones can't influence sexual orientation, but it also seems plausible that it can.

(A similar argument suggests that meta-attraction itself may trigger various mechanisms that are downstream of androphilia, like bonding. I can go into that later when I have time if you are interested.)

> I occasionally get people telling me that it doesn't matter where AGP is causally relevant to late-onset gender dysphoria in males, because we Know that Transition Works and makes people happier. I'm skeptical about the need for understanding the nature of the condition. After all, this doesn't seem to be how we take any other decisions. I don't know why I like programming, yet I work as a programmer, for instance.

Now, there may be associated facts that matter, but often those facts will be several degrees removed from autogynephilia, so that it is easier to just investigate those facts on their own. For instance, masculinity/femininity matters, and if you didn't know anything about trans women, learning about autogynephilia should up your estimate of how masculine trans women are - but masculinity/femininity is fairly visible and so if you do know plenty of representatively sampled trans women, you should have a much better estimate of how M/F they are than about how much AGP matters for their transition.

> actually, I want to explore the point about regression to the mean and group differences in more mathematical detail, because there's more philosophical depth here: regression is an empirically observable phenomenon, but there's also a sense in which the choice of group is meaningfully subjective: do I regress the mean of my immediate family, or my extended family, or my race?

It depends on what information is available, (if the person making the guess doesn't have family info, they will have to go broader; if they have imperfect family info, they will have to mix it together with other priors) as well as on things like heritability (if the variable in question is not heritable but instead e.g. generational, you'll regress based on age relations rather than family relations).

I feel like there are three general options for strategies:

COMPLAIN - try to work within the blue egregore, arguing that they should change their ways to be more aligned with your ways
DESTROY - work together with right wingers (and theoretically also radical feminists, but radical feminists seem unlikely to become a strong force, due to being, well, right wing) to fight back the blue egregore
BUILD - create an alternative community to both the blue egregore and right wingers, which enforces better epistemic and social standards so as to prevent this sort of thing from happening again

All of these strategies seem pretty bad.

The trouble with complaining is that you can't talk sense into people. There's a mixture of reasons for this. One is that there are often powerful strategic reasons for why they've come to their current set of positions. Another is that it's just a ton of effort for them to figure out what your issues are and whether you even have a point.

Aiming to destroy the blue egregore is bad because the opponents are even worse. I don't want to sell my soul to the devil to defeat heathens.

And finally, you can't build an alternative community because nobody really cares enough about it to form that community. Creating a community with better epistemic standards requires you to do something to enforce those epistemic standards, but that's just makes me a pariah among the sorts of people who care about this, because they want to form their own opinion, truth be damned.

Now, why does no strategy work? I think it's because 1) nobody cares about social science because social science is bad, so you can't win anybody over via truth, 2) once you abandon truth, you're only left with policy, and policy conflicts really tend to turn demographics-based. Like you might think that ideally with policy, you'd try to separate factual matters from value matters so they can be engaged with separately. After all, that's what rationality theorems say you should do.

But the trouble is, most of the people engaging in politics, everyone here included, do not directly set policy; instead, they at most determine who the executive should be through voting, and then make a bunch of noise vaguely guiding the executive in one direction or another. The vast majority of the detailed decisions are set by the executive and the people the executive listens to.

But that means you cannot separate factual matters from value matters. It's all about social networks; which human is the executive, and which humans do the executive listen to? But a human packages factual beliefs together with values in an inseparable way. You don't get homo factualis and homo evalualis, you just get a homo sapiens with both factual beliefs and values.

Given this, the main parameter of a person that matters in politics is how much that person channels your needs/wants versus the needs/wants that conflict with yours. Which means that ultimately it just ends up boiling down to pro-trans vs anti-trans.

By degrees such thinkers did arise: and (the general progress of society assisting) the slavery of the male sex has, in all the countries of Christian Europe at least (though, in one of them, only within the last few years) been at length abolished, and that of the female sex has been gradually changed into a milder form of dependence. But this dependence, as it exists at present, is not an original institution, taking a fresh start from considerations of justice and social expediency—it is the primitive state of slavery lasting on, through successive mitigations and modifications occasioned by the same causes which have softened the general manners, and brought all human relations more under the control of justice and the influence of humanity. It has not lost the taint of its brutal origin. No presumption in its favour, therefore, can be drawn from the fact of its existence.
