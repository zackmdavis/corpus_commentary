I was browsing the blog and stumbled on this post. On further reflection with more psychometric practice, I think the answer to the question in the title is "no". The problem with measurement error is that it biases our estimate of the standard deviation upwards relative to its true value. This then leads to a downwards bias on the d, since d divides by the biased standard deviation estimate.

But in the case of g, g doesn't bias the standard eviation estimates for narrow cognitive differences. Yes, g increases the measured standard deviation, but that is due to a genuine increase in the variation, rather than due to irrelevant measurement error.
