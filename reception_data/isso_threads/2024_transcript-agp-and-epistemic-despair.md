#### [-] Tara · 2 likes · 2024-09-10 · http://unremediatedgender.space/2024/Jul/transcript-agp-and-epistemic-despair/#isso-582
> I suspect there's a real effect that if you teach those people about the typology first, they don't end up transitioning, because given current technology where you can't just magically grow a new body in a vat and swap your brain into it, transitioning makes a lot more sense if you actually believe in gender identity."

I'm skeptical that there's such an effect, because knowing about the typology didn't discourage me.

I was somewhat aware of it for years before transition, ever since my own "oh, there's a word for the thing I am" moment. I read The Man Who Would Be Queen a month after starting HRT, and Men Trapped In Men's Bodies a month after that, and neither of them discouraged me from continuing.

What convinced me to transition was a combination of

(1) finally giving cross-dressing a try, and discovering that it gave me a kind of joy and comfort that was totally unlike what I expected it to evoke, and

(2) reading about the experiences of others, starting with Mae Dean's coming-out arc in Real Life Comics and Amanda Roman's "Gender Desire vs. Gender Identity", who described an experience that was very similar to my own but different from what I expected gender dysphoria to be.

I never had any real sense of "gender identity", and I still don't. But I don't see why having one would make transitioning make any more sense: I don't see my transition as making the outside match the inside, I see it as doing the thing I've always dreamed about (or at least something that I've come to realize is close enough to be satisfying).

> I expect [HRT] to make morphological changes, but I feel like a lot of the psychological changes people report are just them more inhabiting their feminine persona more deeply.

I don't think I have much of a feminine persona, but I've still noticed a few specific psychological changes:

Crying comes more easily, and feels cathartic in a way it rarely did before.

Even positive emotions seem "bigger". At least twice, I've looked at a meme and found it so funny that I laughed until tears streamed down my face, and every time I'd look back at it, it would start up again, continuing for at least 10 minutes.


I'm also more conscious of other people's emotions and relationships, which manifests in a few ways. I don't know if these are especially rare among cis men, maybe everyone else would call this baseline, but I'm autistic enough that it felt like I'd unlocked Theory Of Mind 2.0:

The first thing I noticed was when I was listening to my wife tell me about her feelings on some topic and how they'd changed over time - the kind of conversation I'd usually smile and nod through, but this time it was riveting. I was hanging on every word.

Twice, in the middle of heated 1:1 arguments on Discord, I've been struck by the thought that the other person probably said the dumb thing they just said because the topic made them feel a certain way, and that maybe I could turn the conversation in a different direction by expressing sympathy instead of arguing against it. So I did, and it worked.

When I was dealing with organized harassment from a group of people in a community where I'm in a trivial, unofficial position of power, every strategy I came up with boiled down to using that power to disrupt the relationships within the group and make them distrust each other (and it worked!). The first time, it was standard Prisoner's Dilemma style manipulation of incentives, but the second time, it was a lot more Machiavellian than I thought I was capable of: "if I tell them A but refuse to talk about B, then they'll think C, and then if they see D happen, they'll think it was because of E..."


And finally... I became a Taylor Swift fan. No joke, I really think it's connected. Her fans are >75% women, but why? I think a big part of it is that her songs are heavy on emotional narrative. A lot of those songs make me feel empathy for situations I've never been in and never will be in: they're not "relatable" so much as evocative.
