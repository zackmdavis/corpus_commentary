One thing I've been thinking recently as I've started properly studying gender conservativism as part of my research is, people's different levels of gender conservativism probably has massive effects on their experiences with this stuff?

So for example with the gay signalling hazard, this problem only applies in groups that still partly stigmatize homosexuality. In groups that consider homosexuality 100% OK or even extra-high status, you don't have to worry about seeming gay. Probably it's rare for groups to be this way, but presumably greater acceptance of homosexuality may be a reason why some rationalist men join into literal cuddle piles without needing to worry whether they're gay. (Though rationalist male friendships still aren't as intimate as is typical for low-signalling-hazard cultures, but could that be a mix of introversion + adaptation to outside cultures + ...?)

A similar point might apply to autogynephilia. In a very conservative culture, autogynephiles will probably feel a lot more shame, attempt harder to repress, and be less likely to crossdress publically before transitioning. Furthermore, conservative men are much more macho, so when people see conservative autogynephiles, they may be very confused. Meanwhile, liberal autogynephiles recognizably not homosexually-effeminate, but they are probably much more likely to publically do a lot of genderbendy things before/without transitioning, so you get more of a gradient that probably to a greater extent makes people go "oh that's just how he is".
