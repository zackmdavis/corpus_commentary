With all the ones you referenced, I'm surprised you didn't mention Math is Subjunctively Objective:

"When we imagine thinking differently about the quantity, we will imagine the quantity coming out the same; it will feel subjunctively objective.

And there's nothing wrong with that!  If something appears to be the same regardless of what anyone thinks, then maybe that's because it actually is the same regardless of what anyone thinks.

Even if you explain that the quantity appears to stay the same in my imagination, merely because my current brain computes it the same way—well, how else would I imagine something, except with my current brain?  Should I imagine it using a rock?"

and perhaps the more relevant No License to Be Human:

"Reflective coherence isn't licensed for all minds.  It works for me, because I started out with an epistemic gift.

[...][H]umans have received a moral gift, which Pebblesorters lack, in that we started out interested in things like happiness instead of just prime pebble heaps."

It isn't silly IMO, to remind yourself that you, in particular, have also received these gifts, that have had both long histories (over millennia of your non-existence) and short ones (over decades of your existence). It is virtuous to look for and guard the asymmetries, not so much to insist on ones that you know will melt away. Sometimes you just have to

"step back from philosophy, and consider whether what I have just said is really truly true."

(The asymmetry you quoted appears to me to be quite fragile; there are enough pressures (social and intellectual) that they might develop Escher brains that allow them to think like you do, while being completely moral mistaken (as you might be) about, for example, when it is okay to "only temporarily" add to suffering in the world.)

It's sort of fascinating to see one of your ideological opponents describe themselves in almost exactly the same way you have before ("I'd probably have been in their position if I'd read the same books in a different order") entirely by accident. Like the other commentator, I think your attempt at breaking the symmetry here isn't really all that strong; most of your opponents are also likely perfectly capable of tracing back the chain of events that's led them to what they (think they?) know and how they know it, and many of them may well know things that you don't, too!

On a personal level, I think I mostly understand what you're trying to do, and that you do hit on a lot of things that are at least partly true, though I think I mostly end up disagreeing with your conclusions of what those truths should mean for us and how we should act on them. I also think that you're often kind of bad at clearly stating what your position actually is on a lot of these questions, to the point that it's not all that surprising that you're sometimes misunderstood as just hating trans people? I mean, I think most of the Sneer Club readers in this case are basically just applying Bayesian priors here in their own mostly rational way, to use your own preferred terms - most people who take the kinds of positions you do, and use the kind of tone and language you used in the post quoted, do just hate trans people. That you're going to often be reduced down to that straightforward "villain" role based on the commonalities you share with transphobes is pretty much just one more inevitable consequence of humanity's broad tendency towards generalisation. We can lament that people should make more of an effort to "hug the query", but no one has the effort to spare to extend infinite charity to understand all the individual nuances and positions of every single person they consider an ideological opponent; sometimes we're all going to be occasionally reduced down to a simpler picture of ourselves than is actually the reality. I suppose I don't particularly blame most people for being less willing than I am to entertain your blog posts for those reasons.

Also, I kind of think the specific quote of yours being referenced in that thread does reflect pretty poorly on you even by your own standards? The recent rise of FtM transitioners corresponding to the "~5% of girls on the losing end of female intrasexual competition" is a controversial claim to just throw out there without any particular basis, and you don't make an effort to justify it, either there or anywhere else on this blog (to the best of my understanding). When people see you throw out these kind of dubious reactionary statements as if they're straightforward proven facts, that's going to make them less charitable to assume that anything else you say has any real thought and backing behind it, either. I guess I think you mostly have yourself to blame if you feel misunderstood in this particular case.
