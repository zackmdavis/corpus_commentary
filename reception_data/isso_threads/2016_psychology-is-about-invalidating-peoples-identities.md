I tend to feel that a well-designed factor analysis of self-reported data is the type of differential psychology that gives me the most bang-for-my-buck in terms of learning. (Often it is useful to draw on qualitative data for designing it well, and the qualitative data can also be useful for learning.) I'm not sure how this compares to your position - on the one hand, such factor analyses rely very strongly on people's identities, so in a way it could be said to validate rather than invalidate them, but on the other hand factor analyses can be fairly decisive about what sorts of concepts do or do not make sense, and that can often invalidate the concepts people already use.
