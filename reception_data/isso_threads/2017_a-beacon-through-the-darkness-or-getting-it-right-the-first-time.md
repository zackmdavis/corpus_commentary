I can't speak for Mark, who seems to have a more complicated experience, but personally as an AGP I don't at all. In maybe 70% of my fantasies, I have a female body; the body of my partner seems to be irrelevant. I think I fall more into the "genderweak subby catperson-type cluster", which is just culturally - and practically - matched with femininity by default. In other words, using a female body fits the sexual/social/physiological position I want to be in, and there's no further meaning attached to it. But aside from sexual, I'm a dude along all relationship-unrelated sociocultural lines. If I were in a society where thing-focused, obsessive, kinda aggressive, systems-oriented programmers were female-coded, maybe I'd identify as trans just from a perspective of "well, that works better as a signal." But as it stands, from what I'm reading, the classic "trans identity" just has no overlap with my lived experience. And sure you could say that "women can be thing-focused, obsessive etc etc" too - granted! But even then, the female gender identity would be an unwanted extra standing in an awkward conflict with what I'd actually want to get out of it. Become female so you can fight to have the things that males get by default? For what purpose? This is all just so extraneous to what I want, that it just further emphasizes the split between "trans" and "what I am."

I'm not a woman on the inside. I want to be (about 70% of the time, in a sexual context) a woman on the outside.

Anyway, as I understand candaulism from a cursory reading, it's about turning the self into an object of fantasy in a depersonalized sense. But I do want it to be me, I do want to be the one, there's no dissociation there, no outside perspective. Again, just speaking for myself. Though I could I guess see myself kinking on that sort of exhibitionism by proxy, if I didn't have any hope of ever being the one myself.
